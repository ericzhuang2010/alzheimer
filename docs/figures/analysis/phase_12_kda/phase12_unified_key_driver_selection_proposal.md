# Proposal: A Consistent, Data-Driven Way to Select Phase 12 Key Drivers

**Status:** Proposal; not yet implemented  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-14

## Executive recommendation

Phase 12 should use one reproducible selection pipeline for every key-driver figure. The pipeline should begin with **all genes that were actually tested**, not the manually chosen set of 14 genes and not only the 200 genes that were already significant in at least one analysis.

Every tested gene should then be placed into one of three biologically different groups:

1. **Mitochondrial sentinels:** genes that are encoded by mitochondrial DNA or are themselves part of the mitochondrial query in a particular analysis. These genes can show that the mitochondrial program is being detected strongly, but they are less convincing as upstream regulators of that same program.
2. **Nuclear mitochondrial candidates:** genes encoded in the nucleus but known to function in mitochondria. These may regulate or participate directly in mitochondrial biology.
3. **Non-mitochondrial regulator candidates:** genes outside the mitochondrial gene catalog whose network neighborhoods are enriched for the altered mitochondrial genes. This should be the primary group for claims about possible upstream regulation.

The same statistical rules should be applied within each group. The primary regulator ranking should use the third group, while the first two groups should be shown separately rather than mixed into the same “top three” competition.

This approach preserves useful mitochondrial signals, removes the subjective 14-gene gate, and makes the circular and sex/APOE figures directly comparable.

## The problem this proposal solves

The current figures answer different questions because they start from different gene pools.

| Current result | Starting genes | Additional rules | What the displayed genes mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reduced circular ACAT figure | All Phase 12 genes marked as ranking candidates | Primary AD-up or AD-down runs; at least 80% coverage; smallest combined P values; maximum three genes per broad cell network | Strong aggregate KDA signals, often mitochondrial genes that closely reflect the query |
| Sex/APOE figure | A manually predefined set of 14 genes | Conservative support; at least 50% coverage; ACAT ranking; maximum three genes per network | Best-supported genes only within the 14-gene pool |
| “200 all-conservative candidates” | Genes with at least one conservative significant Phase 12 result | Nuclear encoded; not a member of that run's query; overlap of at least two query genes; query size at least ten | A broad set of genes with at least one strict run-level result, not a final ranked list |

The 14 genes are hard-coded in [`phase12_kda_figure_common.R`](../../../../scripts/figures/analysis/phease12_kda/phase12_kda_figure_common.R). They were a prespecified interpretation set, not the output of an algorithm that selected the best 14 genes from Phase 12.

The reduced circular figure is generated by [`visualize_phase12_kda_reduced_circular.R`](../../../../scripts/figures/analysis/phease12_kda/visualize_phase12_kda_reduced_circular.R). Its current procedure does not require a gene to be non-mitochondrial, independent of the query, or significant in a conservative run. This is why genes such as `MT-CO2`, `MT-CO3`, and `MT-ATP6` dominate the figure.

Neither method is inherently wrong, but their labels and purposes need to be separated. A gene that is an excellent **mitochondrial sentinel** is not automatically the strongest candidate **upstream regulator**.

## Plain-language glossary

| Term | Meaning in this project |
|---|---|
| **KDA (key driver analysis)** | A network test asking whether the genes near or downstream of a candidate gene contain more AD-associated mitochondrial genes than expected by chance. “Key driver” means a network-based candidate; it does not prove that the gene causes Alzheimer's disease or causes mitochondrial dysfunction. |
| **Network** | A map of inferred gene-to-gene relationships. A broad network corresponds to a major cell class such as astrocytes, excitatory neurons, or microglia. |
| **Run** | One KDA analysis for a particular fine cell type, sex/APOE group, and direction of AD change. For example, AD-up genes in one fine cell type and one sex/APOE group form one run. |
| **Query or signature** | The mitochondrial genes that changed in AD versus NCI in one run. These are the genes whose concentration around a candidate driver is tested. |
| **Neighborhood** | The set of genes connected to or downstream of a candidate gene in the network. |
| **MitoCarta/core mitochondrial genes** | A catalog of genes with mitochondrial localization or function. Most are encoded by nuclear DNA even though their proteins act in mitochondria. |
| **mtDNA gene** | A gene encoded by the mitochondrial genome, commonly shown with an `MT-` prefix. This is much narrower than the full MitoCarta set. |
| **Self-membership** | A candidate driver is also a member of the query being tested. This can strengthen enrichment without showing that the candidate sits upstream of the query. |
| **P value** | A measure of how surprising the observed enrichment would be if there were no real signal. A smaller value is stronger statistical evidence, but it is not an effect size and does not prove causality. |
| **ACAT** | The aggregated Cauchy association test, a method for combining several P values for one gene across runs. A small ACAT P value means the gene has strong combined evidence across the included runs. |
| **Multiple-testing correction** | An adjustment needed because hundreds of genes are tested. Without it, some genes will have small P values by chance alone. |
| **FDR/q value** | The false discovery rate-adjusted P value. A threshold such as q ≤ 0.05 aims to keep the expected fraction of false positives among reported discoveries near 5%. |
| **Coverage** | The fraction of eligible runs in which a gene could actually be tested: number tested divided by number eligible. |
| **Recurrence** | The fraction of tested runs in which a gene had a conservative significant result. |
| **Fine-cell-type breadth** | The number of distinct fine cell types in which a gene had conservative support. This distinguishes a repeated signal from one driven by a single subtype. |
| **Sensitivity analysis** | Repeating an analysis with reasonable alternative rules to check whether the conclusion is stable. |

## Proposed scientific question

The primary Phase 12 regulator analysis should answer:

> Which query-independent, non-mitochondrial genes show reproducible network evidence of regulating AD-associated mitochondrial expression across fine cell types within each broad cell network?

Two related questions should be reported separately:

- Which nuclear mitochondrial genes show reproducible network evidence?
- Which mitochondrial or query-member genes act as strong sentinels of the mitochondrial program?

Separating these questions prevents a strong mitochondrial structural gene from crowding out a plausible upstream regulator simply because both have small P values.

## Proposed unified workflow

### 1. Freeze the analyses that count

Use the same analysis scope for every figure:

- primary Phase 12 runs only;
- AD-up and AD-down mitochondrial queries only;
- the six sex/APOE groups used in Phase 12;
- validated eligible runs with sufficient query size;
- no pooled group and no combined “AD-both” direction in the primary ranking.

Any alternative scope should be labeled as a sensitivity or exploratory analysis.

### 2. Start from all tested genes

Construct the master table from the complete Phase 12 directional candidate-test results. Do not first restrict the table to:

- the 14 manually highlighted genes; or
- genes that were already significant in one run.

Starting only with significant genes can bias the combined analysis because weak or null tests disappear before P values are combined. The 200 conservative genes should therefore be retained as a useful **run-level support set**, not treated as the universe of genes allowed into the gene-level ranking.

### 3. Classify every result before ranking

Classification should happen at the run level because query membership can change between runs.

| Class | Operational definition | Main interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Mitochondrial sentinel | The gene is mtDNA encoded, or the candidate is itself in that run's mitochondrial query | The queried mitochondrial program is strongly represented; limited evidence of upstream independence |
| Nuclear mitochondrial candidate | Nuclear encoded; in the core or extended mitochondrial catalog; not in that run's query | A mitochondrial component or possible mitochondrial regulator |
| Non-mitochondrial regulator candidate | Labeled `non_mito`; not in that run's query | Best class for upstream-regulator follow-up |

All classes remain available in the master results. The important change is that they are not mixed into one ranking with one interpretation.

### 4. Apply one conservative definition of a valid supporting result

A run-level result counts as **conservative support** only when all of the following are true:

- it comes from the frozen primary analysis set;
- the driver is not mtDNA encoded;
- the driver is not a member of that run's query;
- the driver's neighborhood overlaps at least two query genes;
- the effective query contains at least ten genes; and
- the run-level multiple-testing-adjusted value is ≤ 0.05.

These rules match the intent of the existing 200-gene conservative set. They reduce self-enrichment and avoid interpreting very small overlaps or very small queries as robust evidence.

Conservative support is used to describe recurrence and breadth. The combined ACAT test itself must use all valid tested P values, including non-significant ones, so that null results are not silently discarded.

### 5. Treat missing tests differently from null results

This distinction is essential:

- **Tested with no evidence:** retain the test with P = 1 when the Phase 12 procedure defines a zero-overlap test that way.
- **Not tested or not testable:** store the value as missing (`NA`), not as P = 1.

For the primary analysis:

1. Require at least 80% coverage within a broad network.
2. Combine the available, genuinely tested P values with ACAT.
3. Omit genuine missing values from the combination.

As a sensitivity analysis, repeat ACAT after replacing missing values with P = 1. This is deliberately conservative. Reporting both results shows whether a gene ranks highly only because its missing runs were ignored.

### 6. Correct the combined results for testing many genes

After calculating one ACAT P value for each eligible gene, apply the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction within each broad network and scientific analysis family.

The primary family is the non-mitochondrial regulator analysis. Nuclear mitochondrial candidates can form a prespecified secondary family. Mitochondrial sentinels should mainly be presented descriptively unless a separate inferential question is defined in advance.

Both the raw ACAT P value and the adjusted q value should be stored and displayed. Selection should use the q value, not the raw P value alone.

### 7. Require breadth and stability, not only a small combined P value

A single strong fine cell type can produce a small combined P value. Therefore, calculate:

- number of eligible runs;
- number of tested runs;
- coverage;
- number and fraction of conservative significant runs;
- number of fine cell types with conservative support;
- sex/APOE groups with support;
- leave-one-fine-cell-type-out stability.

**Leave-one-fine-cell-type-out stability** means repeating the combined analysis several times, each time removing one fine cell type. If a gene loses all evidence whenever one subtype is removed, it should be described as subtype-driven rather than broadly recurrent.

Use the following evidence tiers:

| Tier | Proposed rule | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1: recurrent and stable | ACAT q ≤ 0.05; conservative support in at least two fine cell types; result remains nominally significant in at least 80% of leave-one-fine-cell-type-out repeats | Strongest candidates for broad follow-up |
| Tier 2: significant but localized | ACAT q ≤ 0.05, but support is limited to one fine cell type or the leave-one-out result is unstable | Potentially real, but context-specific or dependent on one subtype |
| Tier 3: exploratory | Raw ACAT P ≤ 0.05 but q > 0.05 | Hypothesis-generating only |
| Not selected | Raw ACAT P > 0.05 or coverage < 0.80 | Insufficient current aggregate evidence |

Networks with very few eligible fine cell types should be labeled as having limited breadth information. They should not be forced to meet a criterion that their design makes impossible.

### 8. Rank transparently within each tier

Within each broad network and candidate class, sort by:

1. evidence tier;
2. smaller ACAT q value;
3. greater fine-cell-type breadth;
4. higher recurrence;
5. higher coverage; and
6. gene symbol as a deterministic final tie-breaker.

This ordering is preferable to an opaque weighted score because readers can see why one gene ranks above another.

“Top three” should be only a figure display limit. It must not be treated as a biological or statistical threshold. If only one gene passes the rules, show one. If none passes, state “no gene met the prespecified criteria.” The full ranked table should always be available.

## Proposed figure structure

### Main circular figure

Use two visually separated parts:

- **Panel A — mitochondrial sentinels:** the strongest mtDNA or query-member signals. Label these explicitly as sentinels, not upstream drivers.
- **Panel B — regulator candidates:** data-driven Tier 1 and Tier 2 non-mitochondrial candidates selected from all tested genes under the unified rules.

Nuclear mitochondrial candidates can be shown as a distinct symbol or in a secondary panel. The legend should explain that “mitochondrial” describes gene function/localization, whereas “mtDNA” describes where the gene is encoded.

### Sex/APOE heatmap

Use the same Panel B regulator list selected above. The heatmap should display how those already-selected genes behave across sex/APOE groups; it should not select a new gene list from the old 14-gene pool.

This makes the two figures complementary:

- the circular figure summarizes overall evidence by broad network;
- the heatmap reveals which sex/APOE groups contribute to that evidence.

### Supplementary table

Publish one complete table with at least these columns:

| Column | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Broad network and gene | Identifies the ranked unit |
| Candidate class and mitochondrial tier | Separates sentinels, nuclear mitochondrial genes, and non-mitochondrial candidates |
| Query-member/self flag | Shows whether the evidence is independent of the query |
| Eligible and tested run counts | Makes the denominator visible |
| Coverage | Shows how complete the evidence is |
| Conservative significant-run count and recurrence | Shows repeat support |
| Fine-cell-type breadth | Shows whether support spans subtypes |
| Raw ACAT P and BH-adjusted q | Separates raw and multiple-testing-adjusted evidence |
| Missing-as-missing and missing-as-one ACAT results | Shows missing-data sensitivity |
| Leave-one-fine-cell-type-out stability | Identifies subtype-driven results |
| Network degree or neighborhood size | Helps identify genes favored mainly because they are network hubs |
| Evidence tier and final rank | Makes selection reproducible |

## Additional robustness checks

These checks should not replace the primary rules, but they will strengthen a publication-level analysis.

### Network-hub control

Genes with many network connections have more chances to overlap a query. Ribosomal and other highly connected genes may therefore rank well partly because of network structure. Compare each candidate with genes having similar network degree, or use degree-matched random queries, to estimate an empirical P value.

An **empirical P value** is calculated by repeatedly generating matched random data and measuring how often the random result is at least as strong as the observed result.

### Alternative combination rules

Compare the primary ACAT ranking with:

- ACAT using missing values as P = 1;
- coverage thresholds of 0.50, 0.80, and 1.00; and
- the existing mean-of-log-P aggregation.

A candidate that remains highly ranked across reasonable choices is more convincing than one that appears under only one setting.

### Cross-network evidence

Report how many broad networks support each gene. Cross-network recurrence can help prioritize follow-up, but it should not replace network-specific q values because the networks contain different cell biology and different numbers of eligible runs.

### Biological interpretation after statistical selection

Literature support, druggability, known AD biology, and experimental tractability are valuable follow-up annotations. They should help prioritize experiments **after** the primary data-driven list is produced. They should not determine whether a gene is allowed into the primary statistical ranking.

## What this means for the current gene sets

- The 14 highlighted genes can remain as an explicitly labeled **literature/interpretation panel**, but they should no longer be the gate for the primary sex/APOE figure.
- The 200 conservative genes remain useful as the set with at least one strict run-level significant result. They are not the full tested universe and are not, by themselves, the final key drivers.
- The mitochondrial genes dominating the current reduced circular figure remain useful as **sentinels**. They should not be presented as equivalent to query-independent upstream candidates.
- All tested genes receive a fair opportunity in the unified analysis, while gene class, coverage, multiplicity, recurrence, and stability determine how strongly each can be interpreted.

## Minimum implementation and acceptance criteria

The revised workflow is complete when:

1. one versioned master table generates both the circular and sex/APOE figures;
2. the code contains no hard-coded 14-gene filter for primary selection;
3. every gene is assigned a documented candidate class;
4. untested values and tested null values are stored differently;
5. the 80% coverage rule is applied before ACAT;
6. combined P values receive a documented FDR correction;
7. breadth, recurrence, and leave-one-fine-cell-type-out stability are reported;
8. the top-three rule is used only for display;
9. figures explicitly distinguish sentinels from regulator candidates; and
10. a full supplementary table makes every inclusion and exclusion reproducible.

## Claims the analysis can and cannot support

The revised analysis can identify genes with repeated network evidence connecting them to AD-associated mitochondrial expression. It can compare the strength, breadth, and stability of that evidence.

It cannot establish that a selected gene causes Alzheimer's disease, directly controls mitochondria, or is a therapeutic target. Those claims require independent evidence such as perturbation experiments, replication in another dataset, protein-level measurements, or functional assays.

## Recommended decision

Adopt the unified workflow above as the primary Phase 12 standard. Treat the analysis as data-driven and exploratory until its thresholds are frozen and evaluated on an independent dataset. Keep curated biological knowledge as an interpretation layer, and clearly separate mitochondrial sentinels from candidate upstream regulators in both figures and text.
