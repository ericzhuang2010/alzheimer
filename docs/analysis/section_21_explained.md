# Tutorial: understanding Section 21, “Cell-specific candidate systems link to one sex/APOE-stratified respiratory core”

**Source section:** [Joint Phase 11–12 synthesis](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md)  
**Audience:** readers seeking a plain-language map of the complete Phase 11–12 model  
**Purpose:** explain the integrated hypothesis without turning it into a proven causal pathway

## 1. The central idea

Different brain cells perform different jobs, so they may reach mitochondrial
stress through different routes. Astrocytes may reach it through lipid and
fuel support; neurons through nutrient sensing, autophagy, and redox control;
OPCs and microglia through iron and lipid handling; and several cells through
chromatin, ribosomal, or protein-folding stress. These distinct candidate
systems repeatedly meet at OXPHOS and the respiratory chain. The direction of
the RNA response still depends on sex, APOE, and cell type. The model is
therefore **many context-specific routes toward a shared respiratory
readout**, not one universal AD driver.

## 2. How to read the title

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cell-specific candidate systems** | Different upstream candidates are prioritized in astrocytes, neurons, glia, and vascular cells. |
| **Link to** | Network and cross-phase evidence connects them; causality remains to be tested. |
| **One respiratory core** | OXPHOS complexes recur as the common downstream biological system. |
| **Sex/APOE-stratified** | The response is analyzed separately in groups such as female ε2 and male ε4. |

“One core” does not mean one direction. The same respiratory machinery can be
AD-up in one context, AD-down in another, and mixed in a third.

## 3. Discovery and conclusion

> **“The prioritized findings separate into cell-type-specific candidate
> systems whose neighborhoods link to the same respiratory-chain phenotype:
> astrocyte lipid/metabolic support, neuronal nutrient sensing and autophagy,
> cell-type-specific iron/selenium/redox control, and
> chromatin/ribosome/proteostasis stress.”**

This sentence organizes many individual results into four testable themes.
The common endpoint is respiratory machinery, while the proposed upstream
biology differs by cell type.

> **“The parsimonious joint model is not one universal AD driver.”**

No candidate—APOE, LAMTOR5, RPL11, or another gene—explains every cell and
stratum. A one-driver model would ignore the observed heterogeneity.

> **“It is a set of context-specific candidate systems linked to a recurrent
> OXPHOS core, with direction varying descriptively across sex/APOE strata and
> cell types.”**

“Descriptively” means separate group patterns look different. Formal
interaction tests and independent cohorts are still needed to establish that
sex or APOE statistically modifies the AD effect.

> **“This is an organizing hypothesis for experiments, not a causal pathway
> diagram.”**

The arrows implied below are priorities for perturbation and rescue. They must
not be drawn as established molecular reactions in a paper figure.

## 4. The cell-system table, row by row

### Astrocytes

Proposed candidates are APOE and RPL11. Proposed readouts include TUFM, LDHB,
CHCHD10, and ATP-synthase genes. The strongest bridge is the exact `Ast GRM3`
female-ε2-up/male-ε2-down TUFM reversal within APOE neighborhoods. This axis
links lipid/fuel support to mitochondrial translation and ATP production.

### Excitatory neurons

LAMTOR5, GABARAPL2, SELENOW, WDR82, TMEM147, and RPL11 represent nutrient
sensing, autophagy, redox, chromatin, ER/sterol, and ribosomal stress.
Readouts include ATP5IF1, CHCHD2, PARK7, NDUFB11, mtRNA, and OXPHOS genes.
Superficial cortical and RELN-positive subtypes provide the strongest
recurrence. Several candidates share readouts, so double perturbation is
needed to resolve upstream attribution.

### Inhibitory neurons

RPS15, LAMTOR5, BEX3, and HSPA1A are candidate systems connected to OXPHOS and
protein-quality-control genes. Male-ε2 and female-ε4 AD-down signatures are
prominent. The HSPA1A evidence is low confidence and should not be given equal
weight to the broader LAMTOR5 results.

### OPCs

FTL and ANKRD11 identify a closely shared iron/mitochondrial motif, while RPS15
and BEX3 provide possible ribosomal/death-stress links. FTH1, GPX4, FIS1,
ATP5IF1, and respiratory genes form the proposed response panel. The two
strongest contexts have opposite directions: female ε3/ε3 AD-up and male ε2
AD-down. This supports context dependence, not ferroptosis by itself.

### Microglia

SLC11A1 and RPL11 are candidates; ACSL1, SLC25A37, and lysosomal/mitochondrial
iron are proposed readouts. The compact self-independent KDA call is male ε4.
The strong Phase 11 microglial phenotype is male ε2 and mainly OXPHOS-down.
Those are separate observations and should not be merged into one demonstrated
mechanism.

### Oligodendrocytes

RPL11 leads a multi-gene, self-independent neighborhood involving membrane,
protein import, mtRNA, and OXPHOS genes. This is a broad candidate connection,
not a separately detailed mechanistic module in the current synthesis.

### Vasculature

HSPA1A is linked to HSPD1 and MRPS6 in female-ε4 endothelial AD-down queries.
The effective query is small, so this is explicitly low confidence and should
be treated as a pilot hypothesis.

## 5. The four experimental axes

### Axis 1: astrocyte lipid and metabolic support

Proposed chain:

`APOE context → TUFM/LDHB/CHCHD10/ATP-synthase program → mitochondrial translation and energy metabolism`

Key test: perturb APOE and TUFM reciprocally in matched astrocytes and measure
translation, carbon flow, mitophagy, and respiration.

### Axis 2: neuronal nutrient sensing and autophagy

Proposed systems:

- LAMTOR5 → ATP5IF1
- GABARAPL2 → CHCHD2/PARK7

Mitoribosomal and OXPHOS genes provide shared endpoints. Key tests should
separate mTORC1, autophagic flux, mitophagy, ATP-synthase regulation, and
general toxicity.

### Axis 3: cell-specific iron, selenium, and redox control

This axis deliberately contains distinct cell programs:

- neuronal SELENOW and antioxidant/proteostasis responses;
- OPC FTL/ANKRD11 with FTH1 and GPX4;
- microglial SLC11A1 with ACSL1 and SLC25A37.

They share redox and respiratory endpoints but should not be assumed to use
one molecular pathway.

### Axis 4: chromatin, ribosome, ER, and proteostasis stress

WDR82, RPL11, RPS15, ANKRD11, TMEM147, and HSPA1A may link broad regulatory or
stress systems to mtRNA, mitochondrial protein import, chaperones, and
respiration. These candidates face the greatest risks from broad
housekeeping-network topology or general stress responses, so matched nulls
and toxicity controls are essential.

## 6. What prior work does—and does not—confirm

The preceding tutorials and the [joint synthesis](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md)
cite experiments supporting many individual components: APOE astrocyte
metabolism, TUFM in AD models, Ragulator/mTORC1 signaling, ATP5IF1 neuronal
function, GABARAPL2 autophagic flux, SELENOW–tau biology, ferritin/GPX4 iron
responses, and ribosomal/chaperone stress.

No located study tested the complete context-dependent convergence model.
Thus, cell specificity is a central part of the new hypothesis, not unwanted
variation to average away.

## 7. Limits of the integrated model

- The axes reuse related Phase 08/Phase 11 contrasts, so they are not wholly
  independent discoveries.
- Several fine-cell queries use the same broad networks.
- A network edge has no activating or inhibitory sign.
- Recurrent structural mitochondrial genes can be readouts rather than
  upstream causes.
- Postmortem RNA does not measure protein assembly, respiratory activity, or
  cell-state history.
- Different candidates may converge because of general toxicity rather than a
  selective mitochondrial mechanism.

## 8. The decisive cross-axis experiment

Choose one high-priority candidate–mediator pair from each axis and use matched
human cell systems with bidirectional perturbation and rescue. For every axis,
measure the same core outputs:

- abundance and assembly of respiratory complexes;
- ATP-linked and maximal respiration;
- ATP production, membrane potential, and ROS;
- relevant cell-specific function; and
- viability, stress, and death markers.

The integrated model gains support if distinct cell-specific perturbations
produce the predicted mediator changes and converge on the same complex-level
respiratory phenotype **before** general toxicity. It weakens if all apparent
convergence occurs only when cells are dying.

## 9. One-sentence takeaway

The best joint explanation is not a single AD mitochondrial driver but several
cell- and stratum-specific candidate systems that converge on a shared,
directionally variable respiratory-chain readout.
