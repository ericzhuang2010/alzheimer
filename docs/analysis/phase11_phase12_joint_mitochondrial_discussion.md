# Joint Phase 11–12 synthesis: sex/APOE-stratified mitochondrial pathways and candidate key drivers in Alzheimer's disease

**Synthesis date:** 2026-07-30  
**Pathway source:** [Phase 11 pathway discussion](mt_pathway/phase11_pathway_discussion.md)  
**Network source:** [Phase 12 key-driver discussion](kda/phase12_driver_gene_discussion.md)  
**Intended use:** evidence-organized source document for a later manuscript; not
itself a final paper or a claim of causal validation

## Executive summary: joint discoveries and novelty assessment

Phase 11 and Phase 12 answer different but complementary questions. Phase 11
defines **which mitochondrial phenotypes change, in which direction, and in
which sex/APOE and cell-type contexts**. Phase 12 asks **which directed network
neighborhoods are enriched for those context-specific mitochondrial
signatures**. Their joint interpretation is:

> sex/APOE context × cell type → candidate control system → mitochondrial
> mediator/readout → respiratory phenotype

The arrows in this working hierarchy denote hypotheses to test, not established
activation, inhibition, or molecular epistasis. Both phases inherit the same
Phase 08 AD-versus-NCI contrasts. Agreement across phases is therefore
**internal cross-scale consistency**, not independent replication.

The literature review was refreshed through **2026-07-30** with targeted
PubMed, Europe PMC, and publisher searches for the joint pathway-to-driver
relationships. Primary human, animal, cellular, and perturbation studies were
prioritized. Novelty labels refer to the **specific joint claim**, not the first
publication concerning a gene:

- **Rediscovery / confirmation:** prior work already supports the same broad
  disease or cell-biological relationship.
- **New extension:** the component biology is established, but the
  cell-type-, stratum-, or network-specific relationship is not.
- **New joint hypothesis:** no directly matching primary report was identified
  for the complete relationship. This is a qualified targeted-review judgment,
  not proof of absolute publication priority.

A balanced first experimental wave should preserve both levels of the hierarchy:

- **Candidate control systems:** astrocytic `APOE`; neuronal `LAMTOR5`,
  `GABARAPL2`, `SELENOW`, `WDR82`, and `RPL11`; OPC `FTL` and `ANKRD11`;
  microglial `SLC11A1`.
- **Mediators and proximal readouts:** `TUFM`, `ATP5IF1`, `TOMM7`, `HSPD1`,
  `PARK7`, `CHCHD2`, `GPX4`, `FTH1`, `SLC25A37`, `ACSL1`, `APOO`, and
  `SLIRP`.
- **Phenotype sentinels:** complexes I, III, IV, and V; `MT-ND2`, `MT-ND4`,
  `MT-CO2`, `COX4I1`, `COX5B`, `ATP5F1E`, and respiratory function.

## 1. Analytical framework and evidence hierarchy

### 1.1 Phase 11 defines phenotype and direction

The source cohort contains 276 donors after Yu-compatible exclusions: 142 NCI
and 134 AD. Six sex/APOE strata were analyzed across 54 fine cell types:

| Stratum | AD donors | NCI donors |
|---|---:|---:|
| Female ε2 carrier | 8 | 17 |
| Female ε3/ε3 | 37 | 45 |
| Female ε4 carrier | 26 | 11 |
| Male ε2 carrier | 7 | 6 |
| Male ε3/ε3 | 29 | 53 |
| Male ε4 carrier | 27 | 10 |

Within each stratum and fine cell type, Phase 08 compared AD with NCI using
MAST models adjusted for total RNA count, age at death, and postmortem
interval. A threshold-level DEG required within-contrast BH FDR `< 0.05` and
absolute fold change `> 1.3`. Phase 10 then encoded significant AD-up as `+1`,
no threshold-level change as `0`, and significant AD-down as `-1` for paired
sex/APOE comparisons. Phase 11 tested fixed high- and low-similarity tails
against comparison-specific MitoCarta backgrounds.

Of 324 planned Phase 08 contrasts, 321 were estimable. Male-ε2 CAMs,
`Mic MKI67`, and `Fib SLC4A4` lacked sufficient data in one comparison arm.

Phase 11 therefore establishes pathway recurrence, expression direction, and
stratum/cell-type localization. It does not establish a formal AD-by-sex,
AD-by-APOE, or three-way interaction because the source branch did not fit
those interaction terms and treated nuclei rather than donors as the modeled
observations.

### 1.2 Phase 12 adds candidate topology

Phase 12 used the cell-type- and stratum-specific mitochondrial AD-versus-NCI
signatures as queries of matched broad Bayesian networks. The production
contained:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Planned KDA runs | 1,782 |
| Eligible runs | 1,021 |
| Runs with at least one reported candidate | 840 |
| Significant candidate rows | 10,172 |
| Unique candidate genes | 717 |
| Failed runs / failed validation checks | 0 / 0 |

For each candidate, cumulative downstream neighborhoods of one, two, or three
layers were tested for signature enrichment with an upper-tail
hypergeometric test. BH correction was performed within each run. A
conservative interpretive screen retained primary directional calls, excluded
mtDNA and candidate-self overlap, and required at least two covered genes and a
query of at least ten genes. It retained 694 rows, 200 genes, and 103 primary
runs. Driver membership was reconstructed from the exact overlap list for the
small number of raw `is_signature == NA` rows.

Here, **primary** denotes one of the six exact sex/APOE strata; **secondary**
denotes a pooled sex or APOE signature that reuses primary data; and
`AD_both_mito` is the derived union of that run's AD-up and AD-down genes. The
reported layer is the one- to three-layer neighborhood with the smallest raw
P value for that candidate. **Global** is only a within-run redundancy flag:
a significant candidate is global when it is not within two downstream layers
of another significant candidate in that run.

The Phase 12 circular overview is a descriptive display of the **unfiltered**
KDA summary, not the conservative screen above. It shows the five candidates
with the most significant calls in each broad network; ties are ordered by the
smallest adjusted P value, then the largest fold enrichment, then gene symbol.
Its call counts combine primary and secondary runs, directional signatures,
and derived `AD_both_mito` unions, which should not be read as independent
confirmations or as a causal-driver ranking.

KDA provides neither edge sign nor experimental causality. A candidate may not
itself be differentially expressed, and an `AD_up_mito` call describes the
enriched downstream signature rather than the candidate's expression
direction.

### 1.3 The two significance frameworks are complementary

Phase 10 found no individual mitochondrial gene whose paired-state similarity
or divergence passed directional BH FDR; the minimum adjusted value was 0.743.
Phase 12 nevertheless found many significant neighborhood enrichments. This
is not a contradiction:

- Phase 10 asks whether one mitochondrial gene has a consistent paired state
  pattern across strata.
- Phase 12 asks whether a candidate's directed neighborhood is enriched for
  one within-stratum mitochondrial signature.
- Phase 10 and Phase 12 use different null hypotheses, units, and
  multiple-testing families.

A significant KDA result therefore neither rescues nor contradicts a
nonsignificant Phase 10 gene-level similarity score.

### 1.4 Evidence terminology

Throughout this synthesis:

- **recurrence** means repeated gene-by-cell-type or candidate-run
  observations, not biological replicates;
- **convergence** means compatible evidence from a different endpoint,
  tissue, cohort, or model;
- **cross-scale consistency** means compatible Phase 11 and Phase 12 results
  within the same analytic chain;
- **candidate-self-independent** means that the KDA candidate was not a member
  of the effective mitochondrial query for that run; it does not mean an
  independent cohort, network, or experiment; and
- **replication** is reserved for the same directional result in an
  independent cohort or independently inferred network.

## 2. AD-associated OXPHOS direction varies across sex/APOE strata — [New descriptive pathway discovery and same-resource extension of Yu]

**Discovery.** AD-associated mitochondrial transcription varies descriptively
across sex/APOE strata and cell types: female ε2 is predominantly OXPHOS-up,
male ε2 and female ε4 are predominantly OXPHOS-down, and male ε4 is mixed with
selected excitatory-neuron increases. Male ε2 has the largest mitochondrial
DEG burden.

**Conclusion.** The complete directional pattern is the principal Phase 11
discovery and the context in which all Phase 12 neighborhoods should be
interpreted. Prior work supports sex-, APOE-, and cell-dependent metabolic
heterogeneity, but the targeted review found no independent study reproducing
the full female-ε2-up/male-ε2-down/female-ε4-down/male-ε4-mixed AD contrast.
The large male-ε2 mitochondrial subset extends the parent transcriptome-wide
result in the same data resource. Both findings remain descriptive until
donor-aware interaction testing and independent-cohort replication.

**Data-driven evidence.**

| Stratum | Mitochondrial DEG occurrences / tested | AD up / down | OXPHOS occurrences / tested | OXPHOS up / down |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Female ε2 | 1,128 / 37,647 | 935 / 193 | 403 / 3,736 | 398 / 5 |
| Female ε3/ε3 | 821 / 36,775 | 554 / 267 | 332 / 3,677 | 319 / 13 |
| Female ε4 | 1,633 / 37,006 | 269 / 1,364 | 324 / 3,641 | 50 / 274 |
| Male ε2 | 3,753 / 35,380 | 1,613 / 2,140 | 659 / 3,505 | 95 / 564 |
| Male ε3/ε3 | 869 / 35,304 | 377 / 492 | 171 / 3,534 | 85 / 86 |
| Male ε4 | 1,058 / 35,290 | 510 / 548 | 227 / 3,589 | 141 / 86 |

The effect is not an mtDNA-only artifact. After excluding mtDNA-encoded
subunits, nuclear OXPHOS occurrences remain strongly upward in female ε2
(`258 up / 4 down`) and downward in female ε4 (`17 / 234`) and male ε2
(`77 / 444`). The clearest paired pattern is APOE ε2: 153 OXPHOS
gene–cell-type occurrences are female-up/male-down. `Exc L3-4 RORB CUX2`
contains 33 of these reversals—31 nuclear encoded plus `MT-ND2` and
`MT-ND4`. Female-ε4/male-ε4 reversals concentrate in `Exc RELN CHD7`, while
female-ε4-only loss is prominent in `Exc L2-3 CBLN2 LINC02306`.

**Prior work and interpretation.** The parent transcriptome-wide ROSMAP
analysis found extensive APOE-genotype-dependent sex differences
([Yu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.71463)); this mitochondrial
restriction supplies pathway specificity and direction but is not an
independent dataset. A separate sex-stratified single-cell reanalysis reported
female-down/male-up mitochondrial and coupled electron/ATP programs in
entorhinal neurons
([Belonwu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-021-02591-8)), which
resembles selected ε4 populations but conflicts with the ε2 direction.

Clinical and metabolic studies justify stratification without reproducing the
local contrast. APOE-related clinical effects vary with sex and age
([Altmann et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.24135);
[Neu et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2017.2188)).
Female APOE4 carriers showed greater hypometabolism in one imaging study
([Sampedro et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.18632/oncotarget.5185)), and sex
and APOE4 affected brain high-energy phosphate ratios in midlife adults
([Jett et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0281302)).
Human iPSC-derived AD models also show cell- and sex-dependent mitochondrial
phenotypes
([Flannagan et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2023.1201015)), and a
2026 APOE-targeted mouse study found sex- and diet-dependent effects on
mitochondrial function
([Johnson et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1096/fba.2026-00121)).
Female human-APOE2 mouse brain had the most robust glucose-metabolic profile,
but that experiment did not include male APOE2 animals or an AD contrast
([Wu et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2262-17.2018)).

**Limits and decisive test.** Male ε2 contains only 7 AD and 6 NCI donors, and
the source model does not model donors as biological replicates. Build
donor-by-cell-type pseudobulk profiles and fit AD-by-sex within APOE, AD-by-APOE
within sex, and—where estimable—the three-way interaction. Replicate the
direction-aware OXPHOS result in another brain cohort before attaching
protective or pathogenic meaning to any stratum.

## 3. OXPHOS and the respiratory chain form the shared pathway/network core — [Rediscovery plus new cross-scale topology extension]

**Discovery.** Phase 11 and Phase 12 converge on complexes I, III, IV, and V as
the dominant mitochondrial endpoint: OXPHOS occupies every Phase 11 divergent
tail, while mtDNA and structural respiratory genes dominate recurrent Phase 12
neighborhoods.

**Conclusion.** Mitochondrial and OXPHOS dysfunction in AD are established.
The new contribution is a cell-type- and sex/APOE-resolved chain from
directional pathway phenotype to candidate network topology. The joint
evidence supports a respiratory sentinel program, not the claim that each
structural subunit is an upstream therapeutic driver.

**Data-driven evidence.**

| Phase 11 comparison | OXPHOS genes in 200-gene low tail | Fold enrichment | Tail BH FDR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Female versus male, all APOE | 53 | 2.47 | 9.36×10^-14 |
| ε2 versus ε3/ε3, both sexes | 53 | 2.50 | 5.42×10^-14 |
| ε4 versus ε3/ε3, both sexes | 48 | 2.23 | 1.21×10^-9 |
| Female versus male, ε2 | 56 | 2.66 | 1.18×10^-16 |
| Female versus male, ε3/ε3 | 49 | 2.33 | 6.63×10^-11 |
| Female versus male, ε4 | 47 | 2.13 | 1.99×10^-8 |

Thirty-five OXPHOS genes occur in all six low tails, and Complex IV contributes
11 or 12 of its 12 measured members to every comparison. Phase 12 also
recovers the same endpoint at the topology level: `MT-CO2` has 550 calls,
`MT-ND4` 402, `MT-CO3` 399, and `MT-CYB` 397. However, 3,036 of all 10,172 KDA
rows have an mtDNA driver and 5,349 contain the candidate in its own query.
`COX4I1` appears 169 times but is a query member in 149 calls.

The [Phase 12 circular overview](../../results/figures/analysis/phase12_kda/phase12_kda_circular.png)
makes this structural dominance visually explicit. Of its 35 displayed
network-by-candidate sectors, 28 have mtDNA-encoded candidates and 29 are
structural respiratory-gene sectors when nuclear `COX4I1` is included. The
unique structural genes shown are `MT-ATP6`, `MT-CO2`, `MT-CO3`, `MT-CYB`,
`MT-ND1`, `MT-ND4`, `MT-ND4L`, `MT-ND5`, and `COX4I1`. `MT-CO2` occurs in
the top five of all seven broad networks, and every displayed excitatory,
inhibitory, and vascular candidate is mtDNA encoded. The oligodendrocyte
`COX4I1` sector ranks fourth with eight calls, but all eight contain `COX4I1`
in the query and all eight are global. Thus the figure strengthens the shared
respiratory-sentinel interpretation; it does not make the structural genes
upstream or candidate-self-independent regulators.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Laser-captured posterior-cingulate neurons
showed lower electron-transport and energy-metabolism transcripts in AD
([Liang et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0709259105)), while whole
hippocampus and isolated pyramidal neurons produced opposite OXPHOS directions
in another study, emphasizing cell composition
([Rice et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3233/JAD-142937)). Human biochemical
and proteomic studies report altered respiratory enzymes, reduced Complex I
protein or respiratory capacity, and early ATP-synthase oxidation
([Bubber et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20474);
[Trumpff et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e09353);
[Troutwine et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2022.105781);
[Terni et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1750-3639.2009.00266.x)).
A cross-tissue proteomic study likewise identified a broad mitochondrial AD
signature ([Wang et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-020-00384-6)).

**Limits and decisive test.** mtDNA polycistronic transcription, mitochondrial
mass, RNA quality, candidate-self-membership, and repeated broad-network motifs
can inflate structural recurrence. Rerun KDA with mtDNA genes retained in the
query but excluded as candidates, use degree/expression/mitochondrial-annotation
matched empirical nulls, and measure complex abundance and activity rather than
RNA alone.

## 4. Positive mitochondrial similarity mostly represents shared below-threshold response — [New analytical clarification]

**Discovery.** The most positive Phase 10 mitochondrial similarity scores and
their apparent pathway enrichments are driven overwhelmingly by gene–cell-type
pairs with no threshold-level DEG in either comparator.

**Conclusion.** “High similarity” should be interpreted as relative stability
or shared absence of threshold-level change, not shared activation or
repression. This clarification prevents a publishable pathway figure from
being described more strongly than its underlying states allow.

**Data-driven evidence.** None of the positive similarity scores is
gene-level significant. For pathways appearing in the high tails, including
TCA-cycle and import/homeostasis annotations, 97%–99% of contributing paired
states are `(0,0)`. By contrast, the low tails contain the recurrent OXPHOS
concentration described above.

**Prior work and interpretation.** This is an analysis-specific interpretive
result rather than a claim requiring external biological confirmation. It
reflects the thresholded `-1/0/+1` encoding: two small but nonzero effects and
two true null effects are both represented as `(0,0)`.

**Limits and decisive test.** Replace threshold-only similarity with
donor-aware continuous effect estimates and direction-aware ranked gene-set
tests. Until then, use “stable/below threshold” rather than “concordantly
regulated” for high-tail pathways.

## 5. Nuclear and mtDNA OXPHOS components may become discordant — [AD precedent plus new stratified cell-type hypothesis]

**Discovery.** Selected male strata show nuclear OXPHOS decreases alongside
mtDNA-subunit increases, while Phase 12 contains recurrent mtDNA-heavy
neighborhoods, including the excitatory `WDR82` motif.

**Conclusion.** Mitonuclear imbalance is not new in AD, but the data nominate
a new sex/APOE-stratified, cell-type-resolved discordance hypothesis. They do
not yet demonstrate a fine-cell-specific state. The same aggregate patterns
can arise from compensatory mtRNA transcription, altered mitochondrial
content, survivor-state bias, RNA quality, or fixed network topology.

**Data-driven evidence.** In male ε3/ε3, nuclear OXPHOS occurrences are
`11 up / 75 down`, whereas mtDNA subunits are `74 / 11`. In male ε4 the
corresponding values are `41 / 61` and `100 / 25`. Phase 12's unfiltered KDA is
29.8% mtDNA-driver rows, and `WDR82` repeatedly covers `MT-ND4L`, `MT-ND5`,
`MT-ND3`, and `MT-ND1` in 21 conservative AD-up calls across 12 excitatory
fine types.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Nuclear Complex I subunits are more tightly
co-regulated with one another than with mtDNA subunits in human brain protein
data ([Trumpff et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e09353)).
Earlier AD hippocampal work reported encoding-compartment differences,
including nuclear OXPHOS loss with relative mtDNA preservation or increase
([Rice et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3233/JAD-142937);
[Mastroeni et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalz.2016.09.003)).
AD/MCI blood showed lower nuclear OXPHOS and mitochondrial-translation
transcripts with increases in several mtDNA transcripts
([Lunnon et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2016.12.029)).
Brain mtDNA quantity and quality also vary with aging and AD
([Klein et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-021-00495-8)).
Experimentally inhibiting mtDNA-transcript translation in human neuronal
models induced compensatory mtDNA and nuclear responses and altered APOE,
amyloid, and tau biology
([Gabrielli et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14275)).
A 2026 study further linked cytosolic `MT-ND4` RNA handling to neuronal innate
immune activation in Aβ pathology
([Pan et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz0887)).

**Limits and decisive test.** Model mtDNA- and nuclear-encoded complexes
separately at donor level, normalize against mitochondrial mass and mtDNA copy
number, quantify RNA localization and protein stoichiometry, and repeat KDA
after mtRNA-abundance and topology matching.

## 6. Astrocytic APOE connects to TUFM and an ATP-synthase/metabolic program — [Established APOE mitochondrial biology plus new exact network extension]

**Discovery.** In the same `Ast GRM3` context where Phase 11 finds a female-ε2
up/male-ε2 down `TUFM` reversal, Phase 12 places `TUFM` in an astrocytic
`APOE` neighborhood containing `LDHB`, ATP-synthase subunits, and
mitochondrial-dynamics genes.

**Conclusion.** APOE-dependent astrocyte metabolism and mitochondrial function
are established. The exact `APOE`–`TUFM` relationship, its alignment with the
ε2 sex reversal, and the proposed hierarchy from APOE context to mitochondrial
translation are new joint hypotheses. This is the strongest exact bridge
between the phases.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11: `TUFM` has 19 DEG contexts across 16 fine cell types and all six
  strata (`3 up / 16 down`).
- Phase 11: in `Ast GRM3`, `TUFM` changes from log2FC `+0.506` in female ε2
  to `-0.939` in male ε2.
- Phase 12: `APOE` has 20 astrocyte calls and seven primary calls across all
  three astrocyte fine types; every primary call is candidate-self-independent.
- Phase 12: `TUFM` occurs in all seven primary `APOE` neighborhoods—four
  directional and three derived union calls.
- Representative Phase 12 neighborhoods include female-ε2 AD-up
  `Ast GRM3` (`LDHB`, `TUFM`, `CHCHD10`) and male-ε2 AD-down `Ast GRM3`
  (`ATP5PB`, `LDHB`, `TUFM`, `ATP5F1A`, `AGT`).

**Prior work and interpretation.** APOE4 astrocytes have altered mitochondrial
dynamics and mitophagy
([Schmukler et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-020-02776-4)),
APOE isoforms alter astrocyte central-carbon flux
([Williams et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2020.104742)), and
APOE4 impairs neuron–astrocyte fatty-acid transfer and oxidation
([Qi et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2020.108572)).
APOE4-dependent lysosomal cholesterol accumulation can impair astrocyte
mitochondrial homeostasis
([Lee et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113183)).

TUFM has direct AD-model relevance: its loss in APP/PS1 mouse brain and
knockdown in cells increased ROS and AD-like phenotypes, whereas overexpression
produced reciprocal effects
([Zhong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202002461R)).
Mitochondrial translation inhibition can also change APOE biology
([Gabrielli et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14275)), making reverse
or feedback causality plausible. No primary paper located in the refreshed
search directly established astrocytic APOE upstream of TUFM.

**Limits and decisive test.** Phase 11 and Phase 12 reuse the same stratified
signatures, and Bayesian-network direction has no activating/inhibitory sign.
Use multiple XX and XY APOE2/3/4-isogenic astrocyte backgrounds with APOE
replacement, knockdown, and rescue. Measure `TUFM`, mitochondrial translation,
`LDHB`, ATP-synthase components, respiration, coupling, mitophagy, and carbon
flux; test reciprocal TUFM perturbation to distinguish a one-way hierarchy
from feedback.

## 7. Neuronal LAMTOR5 connects nutrient sensing to ATP5IF1 — [New joint pathway-to-network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** Phase 11's strongest local mitochondrial candidate,
ATP-synthase inhibitor `ATP5IF1`, repeatedly occurs in Phase 12 neuronal
neighborhoods beneath Ragulator component `LAMTOR5`.

**Conclusion.** Ragulator–mTORC1 control and neuronal ATP5IF1 function are each
established in separate prior studies, but no primary paper located in the
refreshed review directly links LAMTOR5 to ATP5IF1 in neurons or AD. The
combined result is a new nutrient-sensing-to-ATP-synthase hypothesis and the
strongest newly nominated neuronal two-node hierarchy.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11: `ATP5IF1` has 34 DEG contexts across 20 fine cell types and five
  of six strata (`8 up / 26 down`) with eight exact paired reversals.
- Phase 11: it is increased in four female-ε2 contexts, decreased in ten
  female-ε4 contexts, and predominantly decreased across the three male
  strata.
- Phase 12: `LAMTOR5` has 96 total and 38 primary calls across 15 fine cell
  types; every primary call is candidate-self-independent.
- Twenty conservative directional calls span 13 fine types and the matching
  female-ε2 AD-up, female-ε4 AD-down, and male-ε2 AD-down contexts.
- `ATP5IF1` occurs in 28 primary `LAMTOR5` neighborhoods across 11 fine types:
  15 directional and 13 derived `AD_both_mito` calls.
- `ATP5IF1` itself is absent from the Phase 12 candidate summary.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Ragulator acts as a lysosomal
amino-acid-sensing GEF for Rag GTPases and mTORC1
([Bar-Peled et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2012.07.032)), and
structural work places LAMTOR5 in the lysosome-anchored complex
([Mu et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/celldisc.2017.49)).
mTORC1 can regulate mitochondrial activity and biogenesis through translation
of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial mRNAs
([Morita et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2013.10.001)).
Neuron-specific ATP5IF1 loss or overexpression changes ATP synthase, mtROS
signaling, synaptic function, and cognition
([Esparza-Moltó et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3001252)).
These studies make the bridge plausible but do not validate its AD direction.

**Limits and decisive test.** Only 9 of 96 LAMTOR5 calls are global, and KDA
has no edge sign. Apply paired CRISPRi/CRISPRa to `LAMTOR5` in APOE- and
sex-contextualized excitatory and inhibitory neurons; measure `ATP5IF1`,
lysosomal mTORC1 localization, autophagic flux, ATP-synthase activity,
ATP-linked respiration, proton leak, mtROS, and viability. Reciprocal
ATP5IF1 perturbation should test mediation rather than correlation.

## 8. GABARAPL2 connects autophagic flux to CHCHD2 and PARK7 — [Preclinically confirmed AD biology plus new human-network extension]

**Discovery.** Phase 11's subgroup-sensitive mitophagy phenotype gains a
Phase 12 neuronal quality-control neighborhood centered on `GABARAPL2` and
recurrently containing `CHCHD2` and `PARK7`.

**Conclusion.** GABARAPL2-dependent fusion is experimentally validated in an
AD model, and a separate non-AD molecular study supports the
GABARAP–CHCHD2 relationship. What remains new is the human
sex/APOE-stratified neuronal placement, the `GABARAPL2`–`PARK7` relationship,
and the complete three-gene module.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11 mitophagy occurrences are predominantly female-ε2 AD-up (`23 / 1`)
  and male-ε2 AD-down (`14 / 46`), with additional female-ε4 quality-control
  loss.
- Phase 12: `GABARAPL2` has 79 total and 32 primary calls across excitatory and
  inhibitory networks and 13 fine cell types.
- Seventeen conservative directional calls span 11 fine types, and every
  primary call is candidate-self-independent.
- `CHCHD2` occurs in 25 primary neighborhoods across ten fine types.
- `PARK7` occurs in 16 primary neighborhoods across seven fine types.
- Supported contexts include female-ε2 AD-up, female-ε4 AD-down, male-ε2
  AD-up and AD-down, male-ε3/ε3 AD-down, and male-ε4 AD-up.

**Prior work and interpretation.** GABARAP-family proteins are required for
autophagosome–lysosome fusion
([Wang et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1507263112)).
SIK2-dependent GABARAPL2 Ser72 phosphorylation restored autophagic flux and
improved amyloid, synaptic, and behavioral phenotypes in 5xFAD mice
([Dai et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40035-025-00514-4)).
CHCHD2 and CHCHD10 directly associate preferentially with GABARAP-family
proteins and promote aggregate clearance
([Zhou et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2026.2678427)).
DJ-1/PARK7 also participates downstream in PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy
([Imberechts et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awac313)), but no
direct GABARAPL2–PARK7 study was located.

More broadly, mitophagy induction improves amyloid, tau, and cognitive
phenotypes in AD models
([Fang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0332-9)), and neuronal
PINK1 restoration is beneficial in AD models
([Du et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awx258)).

**Limits and decisive test.** GABARAP paralogs are partly redundant, and
phosphorylation-dependent function is not captured by transcript KDA.
Perturb `GABARAPL2` abundance and Ser72 state in excitatory neurons, measure
autophagosome–lysosome fusion, mitophagy, `CHCHD2`, `PARK7`, respiration, and
AD-relevant phenotypes, and test whether PARK7 loss blocks the response.

## 9. SELENOW is network-nominated across neuronal redox/proteostasis neighborhoods — [Preclinically confirmed AD biology plus new human-network extension]

**Discovery.** Phase 12 network-nominates `SELENOW` with downstream
neighborhoods containing neuronal mitochondrial carrier, assembly,
translation, redox, and proteostasis genes in the same strata in which
Phase 11 identifies OXPHOS and redox remodeling.

**Conclusion.** SELENOW is not a novel AD gene: tau binding and protective
effects have been directly demonstrated in an AD mouse model. The new
contribution is its recurrent human neuronal, sex/APOE-stratified network
placement and its connection to respiratory endpoints.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11: ROS/glutathione metabolism is mainly female-ε4 AD-down
  (`11 up / 44 down`) and is enriched in the within-ε4 divergent tail
  (11/200 genes, fold enrichment 2.20, FDR 0.037).
- Phase 12: `SELENOW` has 95 total and 35 primary calls across 14 neuronal fine
  types; 89 calls are global and all primary calls are
  candidate-self-independent.
- Sixteen conservative directional calls span ten fine types.
- The clearest recurrence occurs in female-ε2 AD-up, male-ε2 AD-down, and
  female-ε4 AD-down.
- Covered genes include `SLC25A4`, `PRELID1`, `NDUFB11`, `COA3`, `CHCHD10`,
  mitochondrial ribosomal genes, `CLPP`, and `FIS1`.

**Prior work and interpretation.** SELENOW binds tau, promotes its
ubiquitin–proteasome clearance, and improves tau pathology and memory when
overexpressed in 3xTg-AD mice
([Ren et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06572-0)).
Selenow loss also changes redox tone, respiration, and inflammatory metabolic
switching in macrophages
([Misra et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2022.102571)).
Foundational work identified SELENOW as a glutathione-dependent antioxidant
([Jeong et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0014-5793%2802%2902628-5)).
Those studies support tau/redox biology but not the exact human neuronal
neighborhood or its stratum directions.

**Limits and decisive test.** The Phase 11 redox summary does not prove that
SELENOW drives the female-ε4 signal. Perturb and rescue `SELENOW` in
APOE-contextualized excitatory neurons while measuring tau turnover,
proteasome activity, glutathione/redox state, `NDUFB11` and other predicted
targets, respiration, and synaptic function.

## 10. OPC FTL connects ferritin to GPX4, FTH1, and respiratory genes — [New cell-type network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** An OPC iron/redox neighborhood centered on `FTL` occurs in the
female-ε3/ε3 AD-up and male-ε2 AD-down contexts, linking ferritin to `GPX4`,
`FTH1`, mitochondrial dynamics, and OXPHOS genes.

**Conclusion.** Oligodendroglial iron dependence and AD iron/lipid-peroxidation
biology are established. The exact `FTL`-centered, sex/APOE-stratified OPC
network is new. It supports an iron/redox susceptibility hypothesis, not a
claim that Phase 11 or Phase 12 demonstrated ferroptosis. FTL may be protective
iron sequestration rather than a pathogenic regulator.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11: female ε3/ε3 is moderately mitochondrial AD-up overall, whereas
  male ε2 has the greatest burden and is broadly AD-down/mixed across OPCs and
  other lineages.
- Phase 12: `FTL` has 12 total and four primary OPC calls; all primary calls
  are candidate-self-independent, and no call is global.
- The circular overview also contains `FTL` as the fourth-ranked OPC sector:
  12 calls (four primary and eight secondary), minimum adjusted P =
  2.09×10^-6, and maximum fold enrichment 23.44.
- Female-ε3/ε3 AD-up covers 7 of 22 query genes in a 146-gene neighborhood
  (fold enrichment 15.53; adjusted P = 1.89×10^-5).
- Male-ε2 AD-down covers 12 of 81 query genes in a 123-gene neighborhood
  (fold enrichment 8.18; adjusted P = 2.09×10^-6).
- Both directions cover `FTH1`, `GPX4`, `COX5B`, and `UQCR10`; `NDUFB5`
  occurs in the female-ε3/ε3 overlap. The male-ε2 neighborhood also contains
  `FIS1`, `PARK7`, `ATP5IF1`, and other respiratory genes.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Brain iron, transferrin, and ferritin are
prominent in oligodendroglia and redistribute around AD plaques
([Connor et al., 1992](https://doi.org/10.1002/jnr.490310111)).
Oligodendrocytes obtain iron through H-ferritin/FTH1 uptake—not specifically
through FTL
([Todorich et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/glia.21164)).
Iron overload decreases GPX4 and induces ferroptotic features in an
oligodendrocyte cell model
([Li et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11064-022-03807-6)), while primary
OPC ferroptosis can contribute to experimental white-matter injury
([Shen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-022-04712-0)).
Human AD tissue shows altered ferritin, lipid peroxidation, and ferroptosis
suppressors
([Ashraf et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2020.101494);
[Thorwald et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14541)).

**Limits and decisive test.** The two primary directional calls have opposite
directions and `FTL` is never global. Perturb and rescue FTL in OPCs under
controlled iron loading; measure labile iron, FTH1/FTL composition, GPX4,
lipid peroxidation, ferroptosis sensitivity, respiration, differentiation,
and myelination. Rescue by ferroptosis inhibitors would be required before
using a ferroptosis mechanism.

## 11. OPC ANKRD11 marks the same iron/mitochondrial network motif — [New cell-type network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** `ANKRD11` produces near-identical covered mitochondrial query
genes to `FTL` within the same OPC network motif and the same female-ε3/ε3
AD-up and male-ε2 AD-down contexts; the full cumulative neighborhoods are not
identical.

**Conclusion.** ANKRD11 has established chromatin and neuronal-development
functions but no directly supported OPC iron, GPX4, or mitochondrial mechanism.
It may be an upstream chromatin control point or an alternative representative
of the same fixed OPC motif. The latter is more parsimonious until joint
perturbation distinguishes them.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `ANKRD11` has 12 total and four primary calls; every call is global and all
  primary calls are candidate-self-independent.
- The circular overview contains `ANKRD11` as the fifth-ranked OPC sector:
  12 calls (four primary and eight secondary), minimum adjusted P =
  8.81×10^-6, and maximum fold enrichment 16.48.
- Female-ε3/ε3 AD-up covers 7 of 22 genes (adjusted P = 9.37×10^-5).
- Male-ε2 AD-down covers 13 of 81 genes (adjusted P = 8.81×10^-6).
- Covered genes include `FTH1`, `GPX4`, `PARK7`, `FIS1`, `ATP5IF1`, and
  respiratory-chain genes, closely matching the FTL program.
- The complete ANKRD11 neighborhoods contain 196 and 172 genes in the two
  directional contexts, compared with 146 and 123 genes for FTL; it is the
  mitochondrial overlaps, not the full neighborhoods, that are near-identical.

**Prior work and interpretation.** ANKRD11 regulates chromatin, neuronal
differentiation, dendrite development, and BDNF/TrkB signaling
([Ka and Kim, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2017.12.008)).
The refreshed search found no primary study establishing an OPC iron or
mitochondrial mechanism. The Phase 11 context supplies phenotype direction but
does not decide whether ANKRD11 or FTL is upstream.

**Limits and decisive test.** Compare single and combined `ANKRD11` and `FTL`
perturbations in OPCs. Test chromatin accessibility and occupancy alongside
iron/redox, respiratory, and differentiation readouts. If both perturbations
produce indistinguishable downstream changes, network redundancy rather than
two mechanisms is favored.

## 12. Microglial SLC11A1 connects ACSL1 to mitochondrial iron importer SLC25A37 — [Expression rediscovery plus new network extension]

**Discovery.** Phase 12 identifies a compact male-ε4 `Mic P2RY12`
`SLC11A1` neighborhood containing lipid-droplet enzyme `ACSL1` and
mitochondrial iron importer `SLC25A37`.

**Conclusion.** The individual components have external support of differing
strength:
SLC11A1 is associated with inflammatory AD microglia, ACSL1 marks
APOE4-enriched lipid-droplet microglia, and SLC25A37 transports mitochondrial
iron. The exact triad is new. It does not explain the distinct male-ε2
microglial OXPHOS-down signal from Phase 11, and ACSL1 must not be confused
with ferroptosis enzyme ACSL4.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11: male-ε2 `Mic P2RY12` has 12 significant OXPHOS-subunit
  occurrences among 23 tested, 11 AD-down.
- Phase 12: `SLC11A1` has nine calls, including three primary rows in two
  microglial fine types.
- The circular overview contains `SLC11A1` as the fifth-ranked microglial
  sector: nine calls (three primary and six secondary), five global calls,
  minimum adjusted P = 1.24×10^-4, and maximum fold enrichment 616.67.
- Only one primary row is candidate-self-independent and directional; the
  other two are derived `AD_both_mito` unions.
- The candidate-self-independent male-ε4 `Mic P2RY12` AD-up call covers
  `ACSL1` and `SLC25A37` in a four-gene layer-1 neighborhood against a 12-gene
  query (fold enrichment 191.96; adjusted P = 0.0070).

**Prior work and interpretation.** Integrated bulk/single-cell work nominated
SLC11A1 in AD inflammatory microglia
([Zhou et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2147/JIR.S497418)).
Microglial SLC11A1 was directly shown to function as an H+/Fe2+ antiporter
that promotes lysosomal iron accumulation after white-matter stroke
([Qiu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202511482)).
ACSL1-positive lipid-droplet microglia are enriched in APOE4/4 AD brain, and
ACSL1 inhibition reduces Aβ-induced lipid-droplet formation in human
iPSC-derived microglia
([Haney et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07185-7)).
SLC25A37/mitoferrin-1 is an established mitochondrial iron importer
([Shaw et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04512)), and brain
mitoferrin-1 loss affects respiratory function and memory
([Baldauf et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neulet.2019.134521)).
No direct SLC11A1–SLC25A37 microglial mechanism was located.

**Limits and decisive test.** The enrichment is driven by two genes in a
four-gene neighborhood and one candidate-self-independent directional result.
Perturb `SLC11A1` in APOE3/3 and APOE4/4 microglia; separately measure
lysosomal and mitochondrial iron, `ACSL1`, `SLC25A37`, lipid droplets,
phagocytosis, respiration, lipid peroxidation, and viability. Do not infer
ferroptosis without death-mechanism evidence and rescue.

## 13. RPL11 connects ribosomal stress to APOO, TOMM7, and SLIRP — [Established ribosomal-stress biology plus new mitochondrial-network extension]

**Discovery.** `RPL11` is the most consistently upstream, non-mtDNA,
candidate-self-independent Phase 12 candidate and connects ribosomal-stress
topology to the Phase 11 mitochondrial membrane, import, and RNA-maintenance
candidates `APOO`, `TOMM7`, and `SLIRP`.

**Conclusion.** RPL11 stress signaling and ribosome dysfunction in AD are
established. The cross-lineage `RPL11`–`APOO`/`TOMM7`/`SLIRP` relationship is
new and computationally strong, but its breadth could reflect a highly
expressed housekeeping hub rather than a selective mitochondrial control
mechanism.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `RPL11` has 131 total and 53 primary calls across four networks and 17 fine
  cell types; 118 calls are global and every primary call is
  candidate-self-independent.
- In the circular overview, `RPL11` is the fifth-ranked oligodendrocyte sector
  with six calls (two primary and four secondary), all global, a minimum
  adjusted P of 3.59×10^-4, and a maximum fold enrichment of 28.46. This is
  only its oligodendrocyte figure entry, not the full cross-network evidence
  summarized above.
- Twenty-nine conservative directional calls span 16 fine cell types and all
  six sex/APOE strata.
- `APOO` occurs in 15 primary `RPL11` neighborhoods across seven fine cell
  types: eight directional and seven derived-union calls.
- `TOMM7` occurs in four primary neighborhoods across two fine cell types:
  two directional and two derived-union calls.
- `SLIRP` occurs in two primary neighborhoods: one directional and one
  derived-union call.
- Phase 11 had already nominated `TOMM7` for import/mitophagy follow-up and
  classified `APOO` and `SLIRP` as candidate mitochondrial membrane/mtRNA
  mediators.

**Prior work and interpretation.** RPL11 directly participates in the
ribosomal-stress MDM2–p53 checkpoint
([Zhang et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.23.23.8902-8912.2003)).
Ribosome dysfunction and RNA oxidation occur early in MCI and AD brain
([Ding et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3040-05.2005)), and
RPL11 protein was increased in purified AD brain capillaries, although not in
matched parenchyma
([Suzuki et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/0271678X221111602)).
TOMM7 has an experimentally established role in PINK1 stabilization and
Parkin recruitment after mitochondrial damage
([Hasson et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12748)). These studies
support the component biology but not an `RPL11`-directed mitochondrial chain.
RPL11 is a cytosolic ribosomal protein; its recurrence should not be described
as evidence of mitochondrial-ribosome remodeling by itself.

**Limits and decisive test.** Compare each result with degree-, expression-,
and ribosomal-status-matched empirical nulls and an independently inferred
network. If RPL11 remains prioritized, perturb it within a narrow viability
window and separate changes in `APOO`, `TOMM7`, `SLIRP`, import, mtRNA
stability, and respiration from general translation arrest, p53 activation,
and cell death.

## 14. RPS15 is a recurrent cross-network candidate — [New in AD mitochondrial networks]

**Discovery.** `RPS15` is a recurrent Phase 12 candidate across five networks,
and every primary call is candidate-self-independent; it is much less often
the most upstream nonredundant candidate than `RPL11`.

**Conclusion.** The exact RPS15-to-mitochondrial AD topology is new. Prior p53
and Parkinsonian stress biology makes it plausible, but the low global
fraction and ribosomal-hub risk place it below RPL11 for mechanistic testing.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `RPS15` has 123 total and 50 primary calls across five networks and 22 fine
  cell types.
- In the circular overview, `RPS15` is the third-ranked OPC sector with 12
  calls (four primary and eight secondary), all global, a minimum adjusted P
  of 4.57×10^-14, and a maximum fold enrichment of 19.06. This is only its
  OPC figure entry, not its full cross-network evidence.
- Twenty-six conservative directional calls span 19 fine cell types, with
  minimum within-run adjusted P values as low as 4.57×10^-14.
- Every primary call is candidate-self-independent, and individual
  neighborhoods cover up to 19 query genes.
- Only 23 of 123 calls (18.7%) are global, compared with 118 of 131 (90.1%)
  for `RPL11`.

**Prior work and interpretation.** RPS15 can bind and inhibit MDM2, stabilize
p53, and promote arrest or death in non-neural models
([Daftuar et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068667)).
LRRK2-dependent RPS15 phosphorylation contributed to neuronal toxicity in
Parkinsonian models
([Martin et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2014.01.064)).
The targeted review found no primary AD-specific RPS15 perturbation study.

**Limits and decisive test.** Apply the same matched-null, alternative-network,
and candidate-exclusion tests proposed for RPL11. Any perturbation study
should measure translation, p53, viability, and mitochondrial endpoints
together so generic ribosomal stress is not mistaken for selective
mitochondrial regulation.

## 15. Excitatory WDR82 connects chromatin regulation to an mtRNA/OXPHOS motif — [Prior bulk-AD nomination plus new fine-cell-signature placement]

**Discovery.** `WDR82` is an entirely global candidate in one broad excitatory
network whose conservative evidence is restricted to AD-up fine-cell
signatures and repeatedly covers an mtDNA-heavy OXPHOS motif.

**Conclusion.** WDR82 is not wholly new to AD network research: it has a prior
bulk-hippocampal hub nomination, and its chromatin recruitment and
mitochondrial effects have been studied outside AD neurons. Its
sex/APOE-stratified fine-cell-signature placement within a reused broad
excitatory network is new and provides a candidate bridge to the Phase 11
mitonuclear-discordance hypothesis.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `WDR82` has 70 total and 42 primary calls in one excitatory network; all 70
  calls are global and every primary call is candidate-self-independent.
- Twenty-one conservative directional calls span 12 excitatory fine cell
  types and occur only in AD-up signatures.
- The repeated overlap contains `MT-ND4L`, `MT-ND5`, `MT-ND3`, and `MT-ND1`.
- Female ε3/ε3 contributes the strongest stratum-level recurrence, followed by
  female ε2, male ε4, and male ε3/ε3.

**Prior work and interpretation.** WDR82 recruits SETD1A H3K4-methylation
machinery to transcribed promoters through RNA polymerase II
([Lee and Skalnik, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.01356-07)).
A bulk hippocampal expression/protein-interaction analysis previously
nominated WDR82 as an AD hub
([Hu et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3892/mmr.2015.4271)).
Outside neurons, WDR82 perturbation changes OXPHOS, mitochondrial abundance,
membrane potential, and ROS during reprogramming
([Cui et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-023-04871-z)).
None of these studies validates the Phase 12 excitatory relationship.

**Limits and decisive test.** Near-identical mtDNA overlaps across related
fine cell types may be repeated queries of one fixed broad-network motif.
Test an independent excitatory network and perturb WDR82 while measuring
chromatin occupancy, mtRNA abundance/localization, nuclear OXPHOS expression,
protein stoichiometry, respiration, and toxicity.

## 16. Excitatory TMEM147 connects ER/cholesterol machinery to mitochondrial signatures — [New AD network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** `TMEM147` is a recurrent excitatory-neuron candidate whose
neighborhoods contain `PRDX5`, `NDUFB10`, `NDUFB11`, `DBI`, and
`GADD45GIP1`, linking ER/sterol machinery to the same respiratory programs
identified in Phase 11.

**Conclusion.** This is a new AD mitochondrial-network hypothesis. Known ER
translocon and cholesterol functions provide a plausible bridge, but the
targeted review found no primary AD neuronal study showing that TMEM147
controls OXPHOS.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `TMEM147` has 78 total and 30 primary calls, five global calls, and 15
  conservative directional calls across ten excitatory fine cell types.
- Every call uses one broad excitatory network, limiting independence.
- `NDUFB11` is a particularly useful cross-phase readout: Phase 11 identifies
  its female-ε2-up/male-ε2-down reversal in `Exc L3-4 RORB CUX2`, and
  Phase 12 places it under both `TMEM147` and `SELENOW` in that context.

**Prior work and interpretation.** TMEM147 interacts with lamin B receptor and
sterol-reductase machinery and affects cholesterol homeostasis
([Christodoulou et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.245357)).
It is also part of a ribosome-associated ER multipass translocon
([McGilvray et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56889)).
These functions could couple ER protein/lipid state to mitochondria, but that
is an inference. Nicalin, a TMEM147-complex protein, must not be confused with
the γ-secretase component nicastrin; these data provide no γ-secretase
evidence.

**Limits and decisive test.** Shared placement of `NDUFB11` under two
candidates makes upstream attribution ambiguous. Perturb TMEM147 and SELENOW
separately and jointly in the matched excitatory subtype, and assay sterol
homeostasis, ER translocation, the prespecified downstream genes, respiratory
complexes, and mitochondrial function.

## 17. BEX3 connects neural-death signaling to neuronal and OPC mitochondrial maintenance — [New AD network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** `BEX3` connects excitatory, inhibitory, and OPC mitochondrial
programs to a protein with established neural-death signaling activity.

**Conclusion.** BEX3/NADE neural-death biology is established, but its
sex/APOE-stratified human AD mitochondrial topology is new. It is a
second-line candidate because a general change in death threshold could
produce the same network association without selective mitochondrial control.

**Data-driven evidence.** `BEX3` has 36 total and 13 primary calls across three
networks and seven fine cell types, including 26 global calls and seven
conservative directional calls. Covered genes include `ARMCX3`, `ISCU`,
`TIMM17A`, `MRPL20`, `PSAP`, `PINK1`, and `VDAC2`.

**Prior work and interpretation.** BEX3/NADE participates in
p75NTR-associated death signaling. Its suppression attenuated zinc-triggered
cortical-neuron death, and it was induced after experimental ischemic injury
([Park et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.20-24-09096.2000)).
This establishes neural-death biology, not direct human AD or mitochondrial
validation.

**Limits and decisive test.** Test the predicted mitochondrial targets
alongside p75NTR signaling, apoptosis threshold, and nonspecific toxicity in
each nominated cell type. A mitochondrial effect that persists below a
cell-death threshold would support the specific network hypothesis.

## 18. HSPA1A connects stress to HSPD1 in vascular and inhibitory networks — [New, low-confidence network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** `HSPA1A` is a candidate-self-independent stress candidate whose

female-ε4 AD-down query, aligning with the Phase 11 chaperone-loss phenotype
without showing that HSPA1A itself is down.

vascular result has the most direct cross-phase alignment, whereas the sole
conservative call is inhibitory-neuronal. A shared heat-shock or
proteotoxic-stress response is at least as plausible as direct HSPA1A control
of HSPD1.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- Phase 11 contains 37 female-ε4 mitochondrial-chaperone DEG occurrences, all
  AD-down and largely supplied by `HSPD1`.
- `HSPA1A` has 20 Phase 12 calls across vascular and inhibitory networks, ten
  primary calls, and 14 global calls.
- `HSPD1` occurs in all ten primary neighborhoods: five directional and five
  derived-union calls.
- The sole conservative directional call is female-ε4 AD-down in
  `Inh LAMP5 NRG1 (Rosehip)`, covering `HSPA9` and `HSPD1` in a four-gene
  layer-2 neighborhood against a 55-gene query (fold enrichment 71.97;
  adjusted P = 0.0117).
- The best matched vascular primary result is female-ε4 endothelial AD-down,
  covering `MRPS6` and `HSPD1` in a 51-gene layer-3 neighborhood, but the
  effective query contains only four genes.

**Prior work and interpretation.** HSPA1A protein was reduced in AD cortical
samples in an APP-processing interactome study
([Gerber et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40478-019-0660-3)), whereas
HSPD1 and other mitochondrial-UPR genes were increased in familial and
sporadic AD cortex
([Beck et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.2174/1567205013666151221145445)).
APOE4 can also compromise cerebrovascular function
([Liu et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01127-0)).
The differing bulk and local directions are compatible with context
dependence or cellular composition; they do not validate the directed edge.

**Limits and decisive test.** The vascular alignment is small-query dependent,
and HSPA1A is cytosolic inducible Hsp70 rather than mitochondrial HSPA9. Test
paired perturbation in brain endothelial cells and the nominated inhibitory
neuronal context, with HSF1 activity, `HSPD1`, mitochondrial proteostasis,
respiration, stress survival, and cell-type-specific function as separate
readouts.

## 19. Male-ε2 PPARGC1B induction may be compensatory; PPARGC1A and SMARCD3 remain unresolved — [New Phase 11 hypothesis not supported or refuted by Phase 12]

**Discovery.** Phase 11 identifies a uniformly AD-up male-ε2 `PPARGC1B`
pattern despite predominantly AD-down OXPHOS in the same stratum. Related
nuclear candidates `PPARGC1A` and `SMARCD3` recur more broadly, but none of
the three appears in a significant Phase 12 candidate row.

**Conclusion.** The leading interpretation is a testable compensation
hypothesis: PPARGC1B induction may oppose, rather than cause, respiratory
loss. Phase 12 does not support or refute that hypothesis because a gene can be
absent through network coverage, topology, query construction, or
multiple-testing power. PPARGC1A remains a dose-sensitive benchmark, and
SMARCD3 remains exploratory.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- `PPARGC1B` is significant in 11 cell-type contexts, all AD-up and all in
  male ε2, while male-ε2 OXPHOS has 95 up and 564 down occurrences.
- `PPARGC1A` is significant in 11 contexts across nine fine cell types
  (`9 up / 2 down`).
- `SMARCD3` is significant in 19 contexts across 14 fine cell types and all
  six strata (`16 up / 3 down`).
- `PPARGC1A`, `PPARGC1B`, and `SMARCD3` have no significant Phase 12 KDA row.
  This is an unresolved result, not negative perturbation evidence.

**Prior work and interpretation.** APOE4 neurons have reduced PGC-1β together
with respiratory-chain and bioenergetic deficits
([Qi et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2020.108572)).
Aβ lowered PGC-1β in neuronal models, whereas PGC-1β overexpression suppressed
mTOR through a SIRT1/PPARγ-dependent mechanism
([Liu et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10571-016-0425-5)).
Human AD hippocampus showed lower PGC-1α with dementia and amyloid pathology
([Qin et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneurol.2008.588)), and PGC-1α
gene transfer reduced BACE1/Aβ and improved memory in APP23 mice
([Katsouri et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1606171113)).
Conversely, sustained PGC-1α overexpression worsened amyloid, phospho-tau, and
neuronal loss in another AD model
([Dumont et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.13-236331)). No convincing
primary study was found that directly links SMARCD3 to mitochondrial control
in AD.

**Limits and decisive test.** Perturb PPARGC1B bidirectionally in male
APOE2-contextualized neural cells: worsening after knockdown would support
protective compensation, whereas improvement would support maladaptation.
Include PPARGC1A dose-response controls and retain SMARCD3 only if chromatin or
independent-network evidence places it upstream of the mitochondrial
signature.

## 20. The mitochondrial-localized Phase 11 shortlist is mainly a candidate mediator/readout panel — [New analytical synthesis]

**Discovery.** Phase 12 frequently positions the mitochondrial-localized
Phase 11 candidates beneath newly nominated signaling, stress, or
non-mitochondrial candidates.

**Conclusion.** The earlier genes remain experimentally valuable, but their
best joint use is now as prespecified candidate mediators and pharmacodynamic
readouts for upstream-candidate perturbations. This conclusion applies to the
mitochondrial-localized portion of the shortlist; the nuclear biogenesis
candidates in Section 19 remain unresolved.

**Data-driven evidence.**

| Phase 11 candidate mediator/readout | Proposed Phase 12 candidate above it | Primary pair calls | Directional + derived union | Revised role |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `ATP5IF1` | `LAMTOR5` | 28 | 15 + 13 | ATP-synthase mediator/readout of neuronal nutrient sensing |
| `TUFM` | astrocytic `APOE` | 7 | 4 + 3 | Mitochondrial-translation mediator/readout |
| `TOMM7` | `RPL11` | 4 | 2 + 2 | Import/mitophagy mediator; retain direct PINK1 assay |
| `HSPD1` | `HSPA1A` | 10 | 5 + 5 | Mitochondrial-proteostasis readout |
| `PARK7` | `GABARAPL2` | 16 | 9 + 7 | Stress/mitophagy mediator |
| `ATP5IF1`, `FIS1`, `PARK7` | OPC `FTL` or `ANKRD11` | 2 each per candidate | 1 + 1 | Iron/chromatin response panel |
| `APOO` | `RPL11` | 15 | 8 + 7 | Membrane/cristae readout |
| `SLIRP` | `RPL11` | 2 | 1 + 1 | mtRNA-maintenance readout |

The derived `AD_both_mito` rows reuse directional-signature genes and are not
independent confirmations. `ATP5IF1` is absent from the KDA candidate summary.
`TUFM` has 39 KDA calls but is always a query member and never global.
`TOMM7` has eight calls whose primary evidence is candidate-self-containing and
narrow. `PARK7` has six calls, three primary calls, only one
candidate-self-independent primary call, and no call passing the conservative
directional screen. These features favor mediator/readout roles without
proving them.

**Prior work and interpretation.** The component-specific perturbation
literature is discussed in Sections 6–8 and 13. Phase 12 changes the inferred
location of these genes in a proposed regulatory hierarchy; it does not negate
their Phase 11 recurrence or their direct functional relevance.

**Limits and decisive test.** Directed network proximity is not molecular
epistasis. Perturb each proposed upstream candidate and require coherent
movement of several prespecified downstream genes plus a mitochondrial
functional phenotype. Then perturb the proposed mediator and test whether it
blocks or rescues the candidate effect.

## 21. Cell-specific candidate systems link to one sex/APOE-stratified respiratory core — [New integrative hypothesis]

**Discovery.** The prioritized findings separate into cell-type-specific
candidate systems whose neighborhoods link to the same respiratory-chain
phenotype: astrocyte lipid/metabolic support, neuronal nutrient sensing and
autophagy, cell-type-specific iron/selenium/redox control, and
chromatin/ribosome/proteostasis stress.

**Conclusion.** The parsimonious joint model is not one universal AD driver.
It is a set of context-specific candidate systems linked to a recurrent
OXPHOS core, with direction varying descriptively across sex/APOE strata and
cell types.
This is an organizing hypothesis for experiments, not a causal pathway
diagram.

**Data-driven evidence.**

| Cell system | Candidate control systems | Principal mediators/readouts | Strongest joint context |
|---|---|---|---|
| Astrocytes | `APOE`, `RPL11` | `TUFM`, `LDHB`, `CHCHD10`, ATP-synthase genes | Exact `Ast GRM3` ε2 sex reversal |
| Excitatory neurons | `LAMTOR5`, `GABARAPL2`, `SELENOW`, `WDR82`, `TMEM147`, `RPL11` | `ATP5IF1`, `CHCHD2`, `PARK7`, `NDUFB11`, mtRNA/OXPHOS | Superficial and RELN-positive subtypes |
| Inhibitory neurons | `RPS15`, `LAMTOR5`, `BEX3`, `HSPA1A` | OXPHOS and proteostasis genes | Male-ε2 and female-ε4 AD-down |
| OPCs | `FTL`, `ANKRD11`, `RPS15`, `BEX3` | `FTH1`, `GPX4`, `FIS1`, `ATP5IF1`, respiratory genes | Female-ε3/ε3 AD-up and male-ε2 AD-down |
| Microglia | `SLC11A1`, `RPL11` | `ACSL1`, `SLC25A37`, lysosomal/mitochondrial iron | Male-ε4 candidate-self-independent KDA call; male-ε2 Phase 11 phenotype is separate |
| Oligodendrocytes | `RPL11` | Membrane/import/mtRNA and OXPHOS genes | Multi-gene, candidate-self-independent neighborhood led by a nuclear-encoded candidate |
| Vasculature | `HSPA1A` | `HSPD1`, `MRPS6` | Female-ε4 endothelial AD-down, low confidence |

Four experimentally separable axes summarize the model:

1. **Astrocyte lipid/metabolic support:** `APOE` to `TUFM`, `LDHB`,
   `CHCHD10`, `ATP5PB`, and `ATP5F1A`.
2. **Neuronal nutrient sensing and autophagy:** `LAMTOR5` and `GABARAPL2` to
   `ATP5IF1`, `CHCHD2`, `PARK7`, mitoribosomal genes, and OXPHOS.
3. **Cell-type-specific iron/selenium/redox control:** neuronal `SELENOW` and
   glial `FTL`/`SLC11A1` to `GPX4`, `FTH1`, `SLC25A37`, `ACSL1`, `FIS1`,
   and respiratory genes.
4. **Chromatin/ribosome/proteostasis stress:** `WDR82`, `RPL11`, `RPS15`,
   `ANKRD11`, `TMEM147`, and `HSPA1A` to mtRNA, import, chaperone, and
   respiratory endpoints.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Prior work validates many components, as
reviewed in the preceding sections, but no study located in the targeted
review tested this complete context-dependent convergence model. Cell-type
specificity is therefore part of the hypothesis rather than a nuisance to
average away.

**Limits and decisive test.** The axes reuse the same Phase 08 contrasts, and
related fine types query shared broad networks. Test one candidate–mediator
pair per axis in matched cell systems, then ask whether distinct perturbations
converge on the same complex-level respiratory phenotype without converging on
general toxicity.

## 22. Astrocytic RPL13 appears in the circular overview but requires annotation correction — [Annotation-constrained, low-confidence network hypothesis]

**Discovery.** `RPL13` is the one displayed non-mtDNA candidate that did not
already have a dedicated discussion in this document. It is the fifth-ranked
astrocyte sector in the circular overview and recurs across three fine
astrocyte types. Most of that recurrence, however, is entangled with a likely
cross-gene synonym-mapping error between `RPL13` and `MRPL13`.

**Conclusion.** The displayed recurrence should not currently be interpreted
as evidence that RPL13 is a mitochondrial structural gene or a validated
mitochondrial key driver. Seventeen of its 21 plotted calls contain `RPL13`
itself in the effective mitochondrial query. Of the four primary directional
calls, only the female-ε4 `Ast DPP10` AD-down result is
candidate-self-independent. That one result supports a low-confidence
astrocytic network hypothesis that can survive correction in principle, but
the Phase 09 annotation and affected Phase 12 runs should be repaired and
rerun before prioritizing RPL13 experimentally.

**Data-driven evidence.**

- The circular overview reports 21 astrocyte calls across three fine cell
  types: eight primary, 13 secondary, and 19 global. Its minimum adjusted P is
  9.76×10^-6 and its maximum fold enrichment is 128.57.
- The four primary directional contexts are female-ε2 `Ast CHI3L1` AD-up,
  female-ε4 `Ast DPP10` AD-down, female-ε2 `Ast GRM3` AD-up, and male-ε2
  `Ast GRM3` AD-down. Their four `AD_both_mito` rows reuse those directional
  inputs and are summaries, not four extra confirmations.
- Exact-overlap reconstruction shows candidate-self overlap in 17 of the 21
  plotted calls. Three of the four primary directional calls are among those
  self-containing results.
- The candidate-self-independent female-ε4 `Ast DPP10` AD-down call uses a
  layer-3 RPL13 neighborhood of 24 genes and covers four of 25 query genes:
  `COX4I1`, `SLC25A3`, `ATP5F1B`, and `NDUFV1` (fold enrichment 40.66;
  adjusted P = 2.51×10^-4). This is the most defensible RPL13-specific signal
  behind the figure, but it is one primary directional context in one fixed
  astrocyte network.

**Prior work and interpretation.** Phase 09 annotated measured `RPL13` as a
MitoCarta gene through a `unique_synonym` match to `MRPL13`. These are distinct
official genes: NCBI describes [RPL13 (Gene ID 6137)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/6137)
as a cytoplasmic 60S ribosomal protein and [MRPL13 (Gene ID 28998)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/28998)
as a protein of the mitochondrial 39S large ribosomal subunit. A synonym match
that crosses those records can incorrectly admit RPL13 to a mitochondrial
query, making candidate-self enrichment especially easy. This annotation
problem does not automatically disqualify RPL13 as a *network candidate*:
KDA candidates may be non-mitochondrial genes whose downstream neighborhoods
are enriched for a mitochondrial query. The targeted review found no primary
study establishing the specific RPL13-to-OXPHOS relationship in AD astrocytes.

**Limits and decisive test.** Replace synonym-only gene matching with stable
gene identifiers, distinguish `RPL13` from `MRPL13`, and rerun the affected
Phase 09 signatures and Phase 12 enrichments. RPL13 should be removed from the
mitochondrial query unless its own identifier has independent mitochondrial
evidence, while remaining eligible as a non-mitochondrial network candidate.
Then reassess the self-independent `Ast DPP10` result with expression-,
degree-, and ribosomal-status-matched nulls and an independently inferred
astrocyte network. Perturbation is justified only if a corrected,
candidate-self-independent relationship survives those tests.

## 23. Joint limitations and claims that should not be made

The synthesis is stronger than either phase alone for hypothesis organization,
but it does not remove the limitations inherited from either analysis:

1. **No formal interaction test.** Separate significance in two strata, even
   with opposite directions, does not establish AD-by-sex, AD-by-APOE, or
   three-way interaction.
2. **Donors are the biological replicates.** The current MAST branch uses
   nuclei as observations and includes covariates but no donor random effect.
3. **Unequal and small strata.** Male ε2 contains only 7 AD and 6 NCI donors;
   power, query size, and network eligibility also differ among cell types.
4. **Shared data chain.** Phase 12 uses signatures derived from the Phase 08
   contrasts summarized in Phase 11. Cross-phase agreement is internal
   consistency, not independent replication.
5. **Threshold dependence.** Phase 10 reduces continuous effects to
   `-1/0/+1`; most high-similarity pairs are `(0,0)`, and no mitochondrial
   gene passes the directional similarity FDR.
6. **Different and local multiple-testing families.** Phase 11 pathway-tail and
   Phase 12 KDA FDR values test different nulls. Phase 12 BH correction is
   within each run, not across 1,782 runs; selecting the best of up to three
   layers is not separately corrected; and the post hoc conservative screen
   supplies no new phase-wide FDR. Minimum KDA adjusted P values are therefore
   within-run statistics. A significant KDA result does not rescue a
   nonsignificant Phase 10 gene.
7. **Network reuse and edge uncertainty.** Related fine types reuse broad
   networks; Bayesian-network edges have no activating/inhibitory sign and are
   not experimental causality.
8. **Candidate/query overlap.** More than half of all KDA rows contain the
   candidate in its own query, and mtDNA genes are frequent candidates.
   Self-containing or structural-subunit calls are prone to circularity.
9. **Union signatures are derived.** `AD_both_mito` reuses directional genes
   and cannot be counted as an independent confirmation.
10. **Fixed-network motifs can recur.** Repeated overlap across strata may
    represent one stable topology queried many times rather than repeated
    biological evidence.
11. **Small queries are fragile.** Very high fold enrichment from two covered
    genes can be statistically valid yet mechanistically weak.
12. **RNA is not respiratory function.** Transcript changes do not establish
    complex assembly, flux, ATP production, coupling, membrane potential, or
    mitochondrial abundance.
13. **mtRNA is technically sensitive.** Mitochondrial mass, mtDNA copy number,
    cell quality, agonal state, RNA localization, and postmortem effects can
    change mtDNA-transcript measurements.
14. **Network absence is not refutation.** Lack of a Phase 12 row for
    `PPARGC1A`, `PPARGC1B`, or `SMARCD3` is not evidence of no effect.
15. **External studies are heterogeneous.** Baseline genotype, AD-versus-NCI,
    RNA, protein, metabolic imaging, and experimental perturbation answer
    different questions; citation count is not replication count.
16. **Phase 11 tails are large.** A 200-gene query comprises approximately
    27%–29% of an eligible core-mitochondrial background, limiting pathway
    specificity.
17. **Pathway annotations are redundant.** Respiratory and
    neurodegenerative-disease collections reuse many of the same OXPHOS genes;
    multiple enriched labels do not represent independent mechanisms.

Accordingly, the paper should use **candidate upstream regulator**,
**network-nominated driver**, or **putative key driver**. It should not claim
that a candidate is expressed up or down merely because its neighborhood is
enriched for an AD-up or AD-down signature, that a network edge is activating
or inhibitory, or that these data demonstrate ferroptosis, mitonuclear
imbalance, or therapeutic benefit.

## 24. Confirmation and experimental sequence

### 24.1 Confirm the phenotype at donor level

1. Build donor-by-fine-cell-type pseudobulk profiles for the leading
   superficial/RELN-positive excitatory clusters, `Ast GRM3`, OPC,
   `Mic P2RY12`, and endothelial cells.
2. Fit AD-by-sex contrasts within APOE and AD-by-APOE contrasts within sex;
   fit the three-way term only where donor counts make it estimable.
3. Test OXPHOS and complexes I/III/IV/V with direction-aware ranked gene-set
   methods, not only thresholded DEG overrepresentation.
4. Estimate nuclear- and mtDNA-encoded effects separately and test their
   within-donor balance after accounting for mitochondrial abundance and mtDNA
   copy number.
5. Replicate the leading effect directions in an independent brain cohort.

### 24.2 Stress-test the network nominations

1. Exclude signature genes from the candidate set before testing and repeat BH
   correction, rather than filtering significant rows afterward.
2. Compare observed enrichments with null signatures matched for size,
   expression, degree, mitochondrial annotation, and mtDNA content.
3. Analyze fixed network layers and account for layer selection.
4. Test alternative Bayesian-network realizations, uncertain-edge removal,
   edge reversal, and independently inferred cell-type networks.
5. Compare core-MitoCarta and broader mitochondrial-related query universes.
6. Add orthogonal AD genetics, cell-type eQTL/sQTL colocalization, proteomics,
   phosphoproteomics, and chromatin-accessibility evidence.

### 24.3 Use a staged perturbation design

Because the network does not supply edge sign, use paired loss- and
gain-of-function, rescue, and dose-response designs.

| Experimental wave | Cell system and candidate–mediator pair | Primary mechanistic question |
|---|---|---|
| 1A | APOE2/3/4-isogenic XX and XY astrocytes: `APOE` ↔ `TUFM` | Does APOE context alter mitochondrial translation, and is the relationship directional or feedback? |
| 1B | Excitatory neurons: `LAMTOR5` → `ATP5IF1` | Does lysosomal nutrient sensing mediate ATP-synthase control and respiratory coupling? |
| 1C | Excitatory neurons: `GABARAPL2` → `CHCHD2`/`PARK7` | Does the predicted module control fusion, mitophagy, and AD-relevant stress? |
| 1D | OPCs: `FTL` versus `ANKRD11` → `FTH1`/`GPX4` | Are these distinct control points or alternative representatives of one topology? |
| 1E | APOE3/3 and APOE4/4 microglia: `SLC11A1` → `ACSL1`/`SLC25A37` | Is lysosomal iron handling coupled to mitochondrial iron and lipid-droplet state? |
| 2 | Neurons: `SELENOW`, `WDR82`, `TMEM147`, `RPL11`, `RPS15`, `BEX3` | Which second-line candidates survive matched-null/network tests and alter function without general toxicity? |
| Orthogonal | Male-APOE2 neural cells: `PPARGC1B`; dose-controlled `PPARGC1A`; exploratory `SMARCD3` | Is the biogenesis response protective compensation, ineffective response, or maladaptation? |

Prespecified readouts should include the predicted mediator panel, complexes
I/III/IV/V abundance and activity, oxygen-consumption and glycolytic rates,
ATP synthase activity, ATP-linked respiration, proton leak, coupling
efficiency, mitochondrial membrane potential and mass, mtROS, mitophagy,
complex assembly, and cell-type-specific function. A candidate should advance
only if perturbation moves multiple predicted genes, changes mitochondrial
function, and alters an AD-relevant phenotype in the predicted cell context
without nonspecific toxicity.

## 25. Manuscript-ready synthesis

### 25.1 Candidate Results paragraph

> Sex/APOE-stratified single-nucleus analysis identified OXPHOS as the
> dominant divergent mitochondrial program in AD, with predominantly AD-up
> OXPHOS DEG occurrences in female APOE ε2 carriers, predominantly AD-down
> occurrences in male ε2 and female ε4 carriers, and a mixed response in male
> ε4 carriers. The densest reversal patterns occurred in superficial and
> RELN-positive excitatory neurons.
> Cell-type-matched directed-network enrichment then nominated distinct
> candidate control layers above this shared respiratory endpoint. These
> included astrocytic APOE–TUFM, neuronal LAMTOR5–ATP5IF1 and
> GABARAPL2–CHCHD2/PARK7, OPC FTL/ANKRD11–FTH1/GPX4, and microglial
> SLC11A1–ACSL1/SLC25A37 relationships. A conservative interpretive screen
> retained rows from primary directional runs only when the candidate was
> non-mtDNA and absent from the effective query, the query contained at least
> ten genes, and the neighborhood covered at least two genes. This retained
> 694 rows, 200 candidate genes, and 103 primary runs. In the targeted review,
> the complete directional sex/APOE pattern and most exact
> candidate–mediator relationships were not identified in prior work and
> therefore remain hypotheses requiring donor-aware confirmation,
> independent-cohort replication, and perturbation.

### 25.2 Candidate Discussion paragraph

> The joint findings support a model in which cell-specific lipid support,
> nutrient sensing/autophagy, iron/redox control, and
> chromatin/ribosome/proteostasis stress converge on a common mitochondrial
> respiratory core whose direction varies across the analyzed sex/APOE strata.
> The analysis also refines the earlier mitochondrial shortlist: ATP5IF1, TUFM,
> TOMM7, HSPD1, PARK7, APOO, and SLIRP remain valuable candidate mediators and
> functional readouts, whereas Phase 12 proposes candidate layers above
> them. This hierarchy is provisional because both phases derive from the same
> disease contrasts, related fine cell types reuse broad networks, and
> Bayesian-network direction does not establish molecular causality or edge
> sign. Formal interaction models, independent networks and cohorts, and
> context-matched perturbation are required before using “key driver” without
> qualification.

### 25.3 Central paper claim

The defensible central claim is:

> AD-associated mitochondrial transcription differed descriptively across
> sex/APOE strata and cell types, and cell-type-matched network analysis
> nominated distinct candidate systems linked to a recurrent OXPHOS endpoint.

The present evidence does **not** support the stronger claims that the full
pattern is independently replicated, that any Phase 12 candidate is a causal
driver, or that changing a candidate will necessarily be therapeutic.
