# High-priority Phase 11–12 mitochondrial discoveries

Using a strict definition—novel candidate-to-mitochondrial relationship plus credible evidence that the candidate is upstream—I would prioritize six discoveries.

## Highest priority

| Rank | Section | Candidate driver | Why it qualifies |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **§7 LAMTOR5 → ATP5IF1** | `LAMTOR5` | The direct neuronal relationship has not previously been reported. It is the document’s “strongest newly nominated neuronal two-node hierarchy”: 38 primary candidate-self-independent calls, with `ATP5IF1` appearing in 28 primary neighborhoods across 11 cell types. |
| 2 | **§6 APOE → TUFM** | Astrocytic `APOE` | The exact relationship is novel and is the strongest exact Phase 11–12 bridge. It matches the same `Ast GRM3` female-ε2-up/male-ε2-down `TUFM` reversal, and all seven primary APOE calls are candidate-self-independent. |
| 3 | **§8 GABARAPL2 → CHCHD2/PARK7** | `GABARAPL2` | The complete human neuronal module and the `GABARAPL2–PARK7` relationship are new. Driver support is recurrent and candidate-self-independent, while prior AD-model perturbation evidence for GABARAPL2 makes it experimentally credible. |
| 4 | **§13 RPL11 → APOO/TOMM7/SLIRP** | `RPL11` | This mitochondrial regulatory chain is new, and RPL11 is the most consistently upstream non-mtDNA candidate: 53 primary calls, 118/131 global calls, spanning four networks and all six sex/APOE strata. |

## High priority, but validation-gated

5. **§9 SELENOW → neuronal respiratory/redox program.**  
   The gene’s tau-protective role in AD models is already reported, but its human sex/APOE-stratified mitochondrial placement is new. Driver evidence is unusually strong—89/95 calls are global and all primary calls are candidate-self-independent. Its novelty is therefore lower than §§6–8, but its biological validation raises its practical priority. [Section 9](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md#9-selenow-is-network-nominated-across-neuronal-redoxproteostasis-neighborhoods--preclinically-confirmed-ad-biology-plus-new-human-network-extension)

6. **§15 WDR82 → excitatory mtRNA/OXPHOS program.**  
   The fine-cell and sex/APOE-specific relationship is new, and every one of its 70 calls is global. However, WDR82 was previously nominated as a bulk AD hub, and all results reuse one broad excitatory network with an mtDNA-heavy motif. I would prioritize it only after confirming the signal in an independently inferred network. [Section 15](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md#15-excitatory-wdr82-connects-chromatin-regulation-to-an-mtrnaoxphos-motif--prior-bulk-ad-nomination-plus-new-fine-cell-signature-placement)

## Promising, but not yet high priority

- **§11 `ANKRD11`:** highly novel and globally positioned, but only four primary calls and likely represents the same fixed OPC motif as `FTL`; upstream attribution is unresolved.
- **§16 `TMEM147`:** very novel, but only 5/78 calls are global and every call comes from one broad network.
- **§10 `FTL`:** biologically compelling OPC iron program, but no calls are global and FTL could be a protective readout rather than a driver.
- **§12 `SLC11A1`:** novel triad, but supported by only one candidate-self-independent directional result involving two query genes.
- **§14 `RPS15`:** recurrent but substantially less upstream than RPL11 and vulnerable to ribosomal-hub confounding.
- **§17 `BEX3`:** novel, but could reflect nonspecific cell-death susceptibility rather than selective mitochondrial control.

Sections **2–5 and 19–21** are important phenotype, analytical, or integrative discoveries, but they do not securely identify a key upstream driver. In particular, §2 is probably the strongest descriptive discovery, while §21 is the best overall organizing model—not driver discoveries themselves.

If experimental capacity is limited, my first three targets would be **LAMTOR5–ATP5IF1, APOE–TUFM, and GABARAPL2–CHCHD2/PARK7**. I would run **RPL11** first through matched-hub and independent-network validation before committing to perturbation experiments.
