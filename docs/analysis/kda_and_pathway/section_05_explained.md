# Tutorial: understanding Section 5, “Nuclear and mtDNA OXPHOS components may become discordant”

**Source section:** [Joint Phase 11–12 synthesis](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md)  
**Audience:** readers with a high-school-level biology background  
**Purpose:** explain the proposed mitonuclear imbalance and why it remains a hypothesis

## 1. The central idea

OXPHOS machines are built from two instruction libraries. Most subunits are
encoded by DNA in the nucleus, while 13 are encoded by mitochondrial DNA
(mtDNA). These parts must be produced in coordinated amounts. In selected male
groups, the RNA results point in opposite directions: many nuclear OXPHOS
occurrences are AD-down while many mtDNA OXPHOS occurrences are AD-up. That
pattern is consistent with **mitonuclear discordance**, but RNA data alone
cannot show whether the complexes are actually assembled out of balance.

## 2. Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mitonuclear** | Involving both the mitochondrial and nuclear genomes. |
| **Discordance** | The two gene compartments show different or opposite patterns. |
| **Subunit** | One protein piece of a larger molecular machine. |
| **Stoichiometry** | The relative amounts of different subunits needed to assemble a complex correctly. |
| **Compensation** | A cell increases one response in an attempt to offset another problem. |
| **Mitochondrial content/mass** | How much mitochondrial material is present in a cell. More mitochondria can produce more mtRNA without more transcription per mitochondrion. |
| **Survivor-state bias** | Postmortem data represent cells that remained detectable; vulnerable cells that were lost may be underrepresented. |
| **Polycistronic transcription** | Several mtDNA genes are initially copied together into long RNA molecules, making their RNA levels correlated. |

## 3. The title and novelty label

> **“Nuclear and mtDNA OXPHOS components may become discordant”**

“May” is essential. The data show different RNA-count directions for the two
compartments. They do not yet show mismatched protein amounts, failed complex
assembly, or a cause-and-effect relationship.

> **“[AD precedent plus new stratified cell-type hypothesis]”**

Previous AD studies have described nuclear-versus-mitochondrial expression
differences. The possible novelty is the proposal that this discordance occurs
in particular sex/APOE and fine-cell contexts.

## 4. Discovery sentence

> **“Selected male strata show nuclear OXPHOS decreases alongside mtDNA-subunit
> increases, while Phase 12 contains recurrent mtDNA-heavy neighborhoods,
> including the excitatory `WDR82` motif.”**

“Selected male strata” refers especially to male ε3/ε3 and male ε4, not every
male group or every cell. “Alongside” means the compartment summaries occur in
the same broad stratum; it does not prove that the opposing changes occur in
the same individual cell.

An “mtDNA-heavy neighborhood” is a Phase 12 network result containing several
mtDNA genes. `WDR82` is a nuclear chromatin-associated candidate that
repeatedly covers mtDNA-encoded Complex I genes in excitatory-neuron queries.
This is a network association, not proof that WDR82 directly transcribes those
mtDNA genes.

## 5. Conclusion paragraph, sentence by sentence

> **“Mitonuclear imbalance is not new in AD, but the data nominate a new
> sex/APOE-stratified, cell-type-resolved discordance hypothesis.”**

The broad biological concept is established enough to be called prior
precedent. The exact mapping—who, which APOE group, and which cell type—is a
new hypothesis generated by these analyses.

> **“They do not yet demonstrate a fine-cell-specific state.”**

Counts aggregated over gene–cell-type occurrences can show a pattern without
proving that nuclear-down and mtDNA-up changes coexist within the same fine
cell population, donor, or cell. A matched donor-level analysis is required.

> **“The same aggregate patterns can arise from compensatory mtRNA
> transcription, altered mitochondrial content, survivor-state bias, RNA
> quality, or fixed network topology.”**

Possible explanations include:

- **compensation:** mitochondria make more RNA because nuclear-encoded
  respiratory support is falling;
- **more mitochondrial material:** a cell contains more mtDNA and therefore
  more mtRNA;
- **survivor bias:** the cells remaining in late-stage tissue are unusual;
- **technical quality:** mtRNA and nuclear RNA may degrade differently; and
- **network reuse:** the same broad network may repeatedly place mtDNA genes
  under one candidate even if the relationship is not context-specific.

## 6. Interpreting the numerical evidence

| Stratum and compartment | AD-up | AD-down | Plain-language pattern |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Male ε3/ε3, nuclear OXPHOS | 11 | 75 | Mostly down |
| Male ε3/ε3, mtDNA OXPHOS | 74 | 11 | Mostly up |
| Male ε4, nuclear OXPHOS | 41 | 61 | More down than up |
| Male ε4, mtDNA OXPHOS | 100 | 25 | Mostly up |

These are **significant gene–cell-type occurrences**, not unique genes or
donors. The male ε3/ε3 contrast is especially visually opposite. Male ε4 is
also compartmentally different, although its nuclear pattern is less extreme.

The Phase 12 audit adds two observations:

- 29.8% of all unfiltered KDA rows name an mtDNA candidate, indicating that
  mtDNA genes are unusually prominent in the raw network output;
- `WDR82` covers `MT-ND4L`, `MT-ND5`, `MT-ND3`, and `MT-ND1` in 21
  conservative AD-up calls across 12 excitatory fine types.

“Conservative” means the calls passed stricter filters. It still does not turn
them into independent experiments, because several calls reuse related
signatures and network structure.

## 7. What prior studies contribute

[Trumpff et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e09353) found
that nuclear Complex I proteins are more tightly coordinated with one another
than with mtDNA-encoded proteins in human brain. This supports treating the
two compartments separately.

[Rice et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.3233/JAD-142937) and
[Mastroeni et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalz.2016.09.003) reported
encoding-compartment differences in AD hippocampal tissue, including nuclear
loss with relative mtDNA preservation or increase.
[Lunnon et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurobiolaging.2016.12.029)
reported a related blood-expression pattern in AD/MCI.

[Klein et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-021-00495-8) showed that
brain mtDNA quantity and quality vary with aging and AD, reinforcing the need
to measure mtDNA copy number. Experimental inhibition of mitochondrial
translation produces compensatory responses and alters APOE, amyloid, and tau
biology ([Gabrielli et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/alz.14275)).
[Pan et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz0887) further connected
cytosolic handling of `MT-ND4` RNA with neuronal innate immune activation in
Aβ pathology.

These studies make discordance plausible, but none independently proves the
exact male-stratum fine-cell pattern described here.

## 8. The decisive test

A strong follow-up would:

1. estimate nuclear and mtDNA OXPHOS effects separately for each donor and fine
   cell type;
2. adjust for mitochondrial mass and mtDNA copy number;
3. measure whether mtRNA stays inside mitochondria or leaks elsewhere;
4. quantify nuclear- and mtDNA-encoded proteins and assembled complex ratios;
5. measure respiratory activity; and
6. rerun KDA against null networks matched for gene expression, degree,
   mitochondrial annotation, and mtRNA abundance.

Only a matched RNA–protein–function result could show whether the observed
discordance represents a dysfunctional complex, a successful compensatory
response, or a measurement/topology effect.

## 9. One-sentence takeaway

Selected male groups show nuclear OXPHOS RNA tending down while mtDNA OXPHOS
RNA tends up, a pattern consistent with mitonuclear imbalance but not yet
proof of imbalance within individual cell types or respiratory complexes.
