# Tutorial: understanding Section 15, “Excitatory WDR82 connects chromatin regulation to an mtRNA/OXPHOS motif”

**Source section:** [Joint Phase 11–12 synthesis](phase11_phase12_joint_mitochondrial_discussion.md)  
**Audience:** readers who want to understand the WDR82 candidate and the danger of repeated broad-network motifs  
**Purpose:** explain how a nuclear chromatin regulator may relate to mtDNA RNA without claiming direct regulation

## 1. The central idea

`WDR82` helps recruit chromatin-modifying machinery to actively transcribed
nuclear genes. Phase 12 repeatedly nominates WDR82 as a global candidate in
one broad excitatory-neuron network. Its conservative neighborhoods repeatedly
capture four mtDNA-encoded Complex I genes. This provides a possible link
between nuclear chromatin regulation and the mitonuclear-discordance pattern
from Phase 11. However, the same broad network is reused, so repeated calls may
be repeated views of one motif rather than independent cell-specific results.

## 2. Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **WDR82** | A protein that helps recruit SETD1A-containing chromatin-modifying machinery to actively transcribed nuclear genes. |
| **H3K4 methylation** | A chemical mark on histone H3 often associated with active or potentially active genes. |
| **RNA polymerase II** | The enzyme that transcribes most nuclear protein-coding genes. |
| **mtRNA** | RNA transcribed from mitochondrial DNA. |
| **Complex I** | The first major electron-transfer complex in OXPHOS. |
| **Broad network** | A network inferred for a large cell class, here excitatory neurons, and reused for several fine-cell queries. |
| **Fixed motif** | A recurring overlap produced by the same underlying network structure. |
| **Protein stoichiometry** | The relative amounts of protein subunits required to build a functional complex. |

## 3. Discovery sentence

> **“`WDR82` is an entirely global candidate in one broad excitatory network
> whose conservative evidence is restricted to AD-up fine-cell signatures and
> repeatedly covers an mtDNA-heavy OXPHOS motif.”**

“Entirely global” means every WDR82 call survives as the most upstream
nonredundant representative. “Restricted to AD-up” means all calls passing the
conservative directional screen use query genes higher in AD; it does not
necessarily mean WDR82 itself is higher in AD.

“mtDNA-heavy” refers to repeated overlap with several mtDNA genes. Because all
fine-cell queries use one broad excitatory network, recurrence across fine
types is not equivalent to rebuilding the network independently in each type.

## 4. Conclusion paragraph

> **“WDR82 is not wholly new to AD network research: it has a prior
> bulk-hippocampal hub nomination, and its chromatin recruitment and
> mitochondrial effects have been studied outside AD neurons.”**

The gene has both prior AD network precedent and a plausible mechanism from
other biological systems. Therefore, it should not be introduced as a wholly
new AD gene.

> **“Its sex/APOE-stratified fine-cell-signature placement within a reused
> broad excitatory network is new and provides a candidate bridge to the
> Phase 11 mitonuclear-discordance hypothesis.”**

The exact contexts and mtDNA overlap are new. WDR82 could influence nuclear
programs that indirectly alter mtDNA transcription or stability, but the
current analysis does not show a direct nuclear-to-mitochondrial transcription
mechanism.

## 5. Evidence, item by item

- 70 total WDR82 calls
- 42 primary calls
- one excitatory network
- all 70 calls global
- all primary calls candidate-self-independent
- 21 conservative directional calls
- 12 excitatory fine cell types
- conservative calls only in AD-up signatures

The repeated overlap contains `MT-ND4L`, `MT-ND5`, `MT-ND3`, and `MT-ND1`,
all mtDNA-encoded Complex I subunits. Female ε3/ε3 contributes the strongest
stratum recurrence, followed by female ε2, male ε4, and male ε3/ε3.

The clean self-independent/global status supports WDR82 as a candidate.
However, 21 calls do not equal 21 independent experiments because the same
network and closely related queries recur.

## 6. Prior work

WDR82 recruits SETD1A H3K4-methylation machinery to transcribed promoters
through RNA polymerase II
([Lee and Skalnik, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.01356-07)).
A bulk hippocampal expression/protein-interaction analysis nominated WDR82 as
an AD hub
([Hu et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.3892/mmr.2015.4271)).

Outside neurons, WDR82 perturbation altered OXPHOS, mitochondrial abundance,
membrane potential, and ROS during cellular reprogramming
([Cui et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-023-04871-z)).
That experiment supports a possible chromatin-to-mitochondria connection, but
different cells and biological conditions prevent it from validating the AD
excitatory-neuron network.

## 7. Limits and decisive experiment

First, infer or use an independent excitatory-neuron network. If WDR82 again
captures the same mtDNA motif, confidence in context-specific topology rises.

Then alter and rescue WDR82 in excitatory neurons while measuring:

- WDR82/SETD1A chromatin occupancy and H3K4 methylation;
- nuclear OXPHOS transcription;
- mtRNA abundance and location;
- mtDNA copy number and mitochondrial mass;
- nuclear-versus-mtDNA protein stoichiometry;
- assembled Complex I, respiration, ROS, and toxicity.

Time ordering matters. Chromatin changes followed by specific nuclear
programs and then mtRNA/protein changes would support an indirect hierarchy.
A late mtRNA change occurring only after severe toxicity would not.

## 8. One-sentence takeaway

WDR82 is a strong global, self-independent excitatory-network candidate linked
to an mtDNA Complex I motif, but independent networks and perturbation are
needed to show that the recurrence is biological rather than one reused
topological pattern.
